### en-033
Background: Error-reduction procedures play a key role in Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) for individuals with autism due to the negative impact that frequent errors can have on learning. Previous research has investigated correction procedures requiring an active student response following an incorrect trial. However, some intervention guidelines recommend withholding reinforcement after correction to prevent prompt dependence. This study directly evaluated the impact of delivery of reinforcement following an active-response correction procedure during tact training in four children with autism. Methods: An echoic-to-tact training protocol was implemented. A "no reinforcement after correction" (NRC) condition was compared to a "reinforcement after correction" (RC) condition using an adapted alternating treatments design. Results: All participants required fewer correction trials under the RC condition than under the NRC condition. Across 26 sessions in which both procedures were evaluated, participant performance was higher in RC during 17 sessions and equal in 3 sessions. Conclusions: Reinforcing correct responses following active-response correction is effective and does not induce prompt dependence, offering practical benefits for applied behavioral intervention settings.

### en-035
Objective: To evaluate healthcare utilization and daily activity limitations among adults and elderly individuals with and without noncommunicable chronic diseases (NCDs) across sociodemographic strata. Methods: Data from 88,531 household interviews of individuals aged 18 years and older from the 2019 Brazilian National Health Survey were analyzed in this cross-sectional study. Healthcare utilization prevalence among individuals with NCDs was compared to those without NCDs, stratified by socioeconomic and demographic variables. Adjusted prevalence ratios (APRs) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) were estimated. Results: In 2019, 47.6% (95% CI: 47.0–48.3) of the population reported having one or more NCDs. Compared to individuals without NCDs, those with NCDs reported significantly more medical consultations in the past 12 months (APR = 1.21; 95% CI: 1.20–1.23), higher healthcare service usage in the past 2 weeks (APR = 2.01; 95% CI: 1.91–2.11), higher rates of hospitalization (APR = 2.11; 95% CI: 1.89–2.36), and greater limitations in performing usual activities (APR = 2.52; 95% CI: 2.30–2.76). A positive dose-response relationship was observed between the number of comorbidities and healthcare utilization. Across all sociodemographic strata, indicators were consistently higher among individuals with NCDs. Conclusion: The presence of NCDs is associated with increased healthcare utilization and functional limitations across all socioeconomic and demographic groups.

Conclusions: Despite the modest sample size, our findings suggest that ESUS population characteristics remain consistent across countries with differing healthcare system structures and financing models.
