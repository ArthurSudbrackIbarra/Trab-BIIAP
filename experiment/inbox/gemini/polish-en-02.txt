The low-advection regime was exemplified by a problem with constant coefficients, whereas non-constant data were used for the high-advection regime. ### en-012
We present a checklist of Odonata (Insecta) species occurring in a fragment of Seasonal Deciduous Forest (Atlantic Forest biome) in the central region of the state of Rio Grande do Sul (RS), southern Brazil, alongside a list of odonate species recorded within this phytoecological region statewide. In addition, we provide observations on the seasonal distribution of species occurring in the study area. Surveys were conducted across two streams and seven farm ponds located in the middle course of the Jacuí River basin between December 2007 and February 2009. In total, we recorded 49 species belonging to 21 genera and six families. *Argia serva* Hagen in Selys, 1865 (Coenagrionidae) is recorded for the first time in the state, bringing the total number of known Odonata species in Rio Grande do Sul to 183. The species richness in the study area accounts for approximately 26% of the state's total Odonata diversity. Libellulidae was the most species-rich family (22 species, ~45% of the total), followed by Coenagrionidae (18 species, 37% of the total). The checklist for the Seasonal Deciduous Forest in RS identified 83 Odonata species in this phytoecological region (~45% of the state's total odonate species richness). This elevated diversity may be attributable to the dense vegetation structure. Within the study area, 20 species were detected in streams and 45 in farm ponds. Species occurrences exhibited strong seasonal patterns: 88% of species were recorded from summer through autumn, and no species were detected in streams during winter. Furthermore, 70% of species in farm ponds were recorded in only one or two seasons, whereas 65% of species in streams were restricted to a single season. These results indicate that the life cycle of Odonata in southern Brazil is strongly driven by seasonal temperature variations.

Measuring systems characterized by slow gas transport kinetics is often considered simpler than assessing ultra-fast systems. However, when transport processes involve time constants spanning several hours, data acquisition, analysis, and interpretation present major challenges in reliably calculating the governing time constant. In this work, experimental measurements of CO2 diffusion in Na,Cs-Rho zeolite crystals, obtained via the zero-length column (ZLC) technique, serve as a case study for evaluating extremely slow adsorption dynamics. For this system, which exhibits a time constant of approximately 8 h, we demonstrate that applying the partial loading approach is essential to determine an unambiguous time constant from ZLC desorption curves. The standard analysis is refined using a non-linear ZLC model to account for isotherm non-linearity, which leads to concentration-dependent diffusivity. Finally, the analytical approach proposed by Cavalcante is applied to confirm the 3D diffusion pathway within the system.

### en-019
Data from field experiments conducted in table grape vineyards (cv. Festival) in Petrolina, Pernambuco, Brazil, from October 2009 to November 2010, were analyzed to evaluate the impact of plastic overhead covers on viticultural productivity and financial profitability in the São Francisco River Valley. Three treatments were evaluated: uncovered canopy (UC), plastic cover positioned 50 cm above the canopy (PC50), and plastic cover positioned 100 cm above the canopy (PC100). Microclimatic results indicated that higher radiative flux at cluster height in the PC100 treatment contributed to increased yield, whereas the PC50 treatment exhibited reduced radiative flux at cluster height and significantly lower yield. Crop yield under PC100 exceeded that of UC and PC50 by more than 11.0 t ha⁻¹ and 12.3 t ha⁻¹, respectively. Additionally, grapes harvested from the PC100 treatment exhibited the lowest defect incidence, maximizing total gross revenue. Conversely, the markedly lower productivity under the PC50 treatment resulted in substantial financial losses.
