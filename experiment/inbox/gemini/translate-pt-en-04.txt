### tr-031
Meteorological systems that cause storms accompanied by strong winds, such as cold fronts and extratropical and subtropical cyclones, often interfere with sea state conditions. Consequently, sea levels can rise extraordinarily, causing coastal flooding. The years 2015 and 2016 were characterized by the occurrence of the positive phase of the El Niño - Southern Oscillation phenomenon, with very strong intensity. Southern Brazil exhibited higher atmospheric instability conditions, with frequent passages of frontal systems and formation of extratropical cyclones, interfering with sea conditions along the Southern and Southeastern coasts of Brazil. Given the above, this study aimed to evaluate atmospheric conditions during storm surge events on the Southern and Southeastern coasts of Brazil during the 2015/2016 El Niño, using information from Bad Weather Warnings issued by the Brazilian Navy Hydrographic Center. Through these warnings and ERA-Interim reanalysis data, distributions by maritime area, month, and season were analyzed, and surface-plotted synoptic fields were analyzed for the 12 most prominent events. As results, the autumn and winter months recorded the highest occurrences of storm surges in both areas during the 2015/2016 El Niño period. Overall, four atmospheric patterns favorable to storm surge events were identified, based on the activity of extratropical cyclones and post-frontal high-pressure systems.

### tr-032
This article presents part of the research results of a master's thesis seeking to deepen reflections on librarians' practices and identify their perceptions and meanings regarding professional performance and the academic library within the context of cyberculture. The study was conducted in a medical university library, presenting the trajectory and characteristics of this library and identifying, based on semi-structured interviews conducted with eight active or retired librarians, the meanings attributed to librarian practices. The narratives reveal a constant search for professional recognition and appreciation, as well as a concern with maintaining user attendance at the physical space of the studied library. Among the perceived changes in this role, it is noted that the sense of "mediator" gives way to the sense of "educator," and the stance of "waiting for the user" gives way to "reaching out to the user," reflecting an apprehension regarding the future of the library and the librarian's role. Likewise, meanings were identified that may indicate strategies for maintaining professional culture and remaining in the labor market.

### tr-033
The evolution of the rural population and animals of some native Portuguese breeds has shown concerning levels of decline, with environmental and ecological consequences for territorial management, planning, and sustainability, particularly regarding the socioeconomic dimension and territorial cohesion itself. In this work, based on interviews conducted with breeders, companies, and associations dedicated to raising and protecting the Garrano equine breed—used for various purposes—we aim to identify the benefits of adopting a farming system that includes the Garrano, while also highlighting its main weaknesses. The approach is developed considering the environmental, economic, and social dimensions of sustainability. The results highlight the importance of these animals, not only as living genetic heritage, but also in wildfire defense, specifically in fuel biomass control. However, it will be necessary to define a concerted strategy to enhance their contribution to the economy of farms or enterprises, which should be reflected in increased population well-being, stabilization of the animal herd, and territorial sustainability.

### tr-034
The State of Ceará stands out in the wind energy production process due to its privileged geographical location for wind power generation. This work aims to present the environmental, social, economic, and technological impacts arising from the implementation and operation of Wind Farms on the Ceará coast through a comparative analysis between the Beberibe and Fleixeiras I Wind Farms. The main socio-environmental impacts detected were: reduction of fauna and flora, wear of access roads, noise production, decharacterization of the natural landscape, restriction of the right to free movement, and cracks in houses. Economic impacts were more pronounced during wind farm construction, gradually decreasing over the years. Regarding technological aspects, it was observed that the greater the tower height and rotor diameter, the greater the amount of energy generated. This investigation was descriptive and exploratory in nature, describing impacts using qualitative and quantitative methods. Directed studies were necessary to understand the impacts caused by wind energy sources and to suggest alternatives to mitigate the worsening of climate change caused by the greenhouse effect on the planet.

There are many challenges to closing the gaps between scientific evidence and better health outcomes. Knowledge translation (KT) is the movement that knowledge makes within an organizational context toward its effective application. The objective of this study was to analyze the occurrence of KT at the Executive Secretariat for Health Surveillance of Pernambuco (SEVS/PE), taking as its object ten evaluative studies on Health Surveillance programs produced in the Master's Program in Health Evaluation at the Instituto de Medicina Integral Prof. Fernando Figueira from 2011 to 2015. For this purpose, a semi-structured interview script was used, addressing seven managers and ten technical staff members from SEVS-PE. In analyzing this qualitative case study, a KT model was utilized, overlaid with an evaluation use-influence theoretical framework. In eight analyzed studies, evidence was categorized across five stages of KT. Only the stage evaluating knowledge use was not evidenced. Different types of use, dimensions, and levels of evaluation influence were reported. Among the changes that occurred, respondents reported: creation of new agendas, development of protocols, changes in work processes, institution of new workflows, and hiring of new professionals.

### tr-037
This article analyzes the road safety of federal highways located in urban areas in Southern Brazil. It evaluates the occurrence of casualties based on records from the Federal Highway Police (PRF), combined with pavement conditions, road geometry, and traffic signage, according to the methodology of the National Confederation of Transport (CNT). Critical urban stretches were highlighted using geoprocessing software, and accident delineation relied on concepts based on the Safe System approach, which posits that traffic accidents, when they occur, should not lead to severe or fatal injuries. When considering fatalities and seriously injured victims, urban stretches of federal highways in the Southern region were among the most dangerous in Brazil. Most highways in urban areas were classified as "good" regarding state of conservation, but several segments concentrated a high number of casualties, predominantly involving motorcycles. As a negative highlight, São José in Santa Catarina presented the most critical stretch. Of the segments with high accident rates, almost all were located near major urban agglomerations or regional capitals, likely due to the high volume of trips generated by socioeconomic activities.

### tr-038
The family Cactaceae includes 1,850 species distributed primarily across the Americas, with Brazil being the third largest center of diversity for the family, housing 277 species. Within this latter diversity center, the Boa Nova National Park (PNBN), located in eastern Bahia State, Brazil, covers an area of approximately 12,065 ha, including an east-west ecotone where Atlantic Forest areas are replaced by vine forest (mata-de-cipó) and, further west, by Caatinga. As part of the proposal for the 2011 National Action Plan for Cactaceae Conservation, this study presents a taxonomic survey of Cactaceae occurring in the PNBN, aiming to expand knowledge of the local flora and Conservation Units in Bahia State. Material was collected during four sampling expeditions over one year along random trails traversing the park's diverse environments. A total of 10 genera and 15 species were recorded: Arrojadoa penicillata, Brasilicereus phaeacanthus, Brasiliopuntia brasiliensis, Cereus jamacaru, Cereus sp., Melocactus ernestii, Pereskia aculeata, P. bahiensis, Pilosocereus catingicola, P. pentaedrophorus subsp. robustus, Rhipsalis hileiabaiana, R. russellii, Stephanocereus leucostele, Tacinga funalis, and T. werneri. Within the PNBN, vine forest and Caatinga vegetations presented the highest species richness, with eight and five species, respectively, whereas the Atlantic Forest contained a single species.

### tr-040
Considering the particleboard industry, the majority utilizes synthetic adhesives, especially urea-formaldehyde-based ones; in this regard, studies encompassing the characteristics of alternative materials for the panel industry become important, particularly when considering the final cost of generated products. The overall objective of this study was to evaluate changes in the chemical structure of the adhesive that could cause possible modifications in its bonding properties due to the replacement of urea-formaldehyde adhesive with lignosulfonate. For this purpose, the elemental chemical composition of lignosulfonate and its chemical bonds were determined using infrared (IR) and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, both pure and in combination with different catalysts; the properties of urea-formaldehyde and its modifications, such as pH, viscosity, and gelation time, were also evaluated. NMR analysis of lignosulfonate showed the typical pattern generally obtained for lignin derivatives in their chemical bonds. Elemental chemical analysis revealed high contents of Mg, Ca, and Na. The addition of lignosulfonate to urea-formaldehyde caused acidification of the adhesive and a reduction in viscosity values. Gelation time was also affected; however, it was found that the methodology for this type of polymer must be readjusted relative to the traditional method, subjecting it to temperatures above 90 °C. Results led to the conclusion that lignosulfonate can be used as a replacement for urea-formaldehyde adhesive in particleboard production.
