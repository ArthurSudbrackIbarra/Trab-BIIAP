### en-051
In this paper, we analyze the atmosphere as a mass-specific gas mixture governed by the Van der Waals equation of state, incorporating the primary components of air and their respective critical properties (critical temperature $T_C$ and critical pressure $p_C$). By adjusting the corresponding constants (designated as $I$ and $D$), we derive the Van der Waals equation of state tailored to atmospheric dynamics. Next, we evaluate the order of magnitude of the equation's terms and propose a simplified Van der Waals-like formulation dependent solely on $D$, termed the WD equation of state. Additionally, we evaluate the pressure terms related to intermolecular repulsive and attractive forces within air using a physical modeling framework, which independently yields the proposed WD equation. Using this framework, we demonstrate that potential temperature and equivalent potential temperature preserve the standard analytical expressions used in atmospheric thermodynamics under the ideal gas assumption. However, we identify $D$-dependent correction terms in both the virtual temperature formulation and the alternative representation of the first law of thermodynamics.

### en-052
Objective: To analyze the association between the incidence of new tuberculosis cases and the Adapted Living Conditions Index, and to describe the spatial distribution of the disease in an endemic municipality. Methods: This analytical, ecological study analyzed new tuberculosis cases among residents of an endemic municipality in the Northern Region of Brazil. Data were extracted from the Notifiable Diseases Information System (SINAN) and the 2010 Demographic Census. The Adapted Living Conditions Index was derived using factor analysis, and its association with tuberculosis occurrence was evaluated via chi-square tests ($\alpha = 0.05$). Kernel density estimation was applied to map tuberculosis concentration across census sectors. Results: The overall incidence rate was 97.5 cases per 100,000 inhabitants. A statistically significant association was observed between case occurrence and socioeconomic status, with higher economic strata demonstrating reduced disease risk. Spatial mapping revealed a heterogeneous distribution of tuberculosis, with primary clustering in the southern region of the municipality. Conclusion: The strong association between tuberculosis and precarious living conditions highlights the need to address social determinants of health. These findings provide baseline evidence for deploying targeted, equitable health interventions in high-risk, socioeconomically vulnerable areas.

Benthic elasmobranchs, including guitarfishes, skates, and stingrays, are highly vulnerable to commercial fisheries bycatch due to spatial overlap with fishing grounds. In reporting systems, landings for these species are frequently aggregated into generic categories, impeding species-specific stock assessments. Along the eastern coast of Australia, the Eastern Fiddler Ray (*Trygonorrhina fasciata*) and the Sydney Skate (*Dentiraja australis*) constitute common bycatch components; however, data regarding their age, growth, and reproductive biology remain limited. This study estimated age, growth (via vertebral band counts), and reproductive parameters for both species based on 171 Eastern Fiddler Rays (100 females, 71 males) and 81 Sydney Skates (47 females, 34 males). Parameter estimates from von Bertalanffy growth curves indicated that Eastern Fiddler Rays attained a larger asymptotic size but grew more slowly than Sydney Skates (ray: $L_\infty = 109.61$, $t_0 = 0.26$, $K = 0.20$; skate: $L_\infty = 51.95$, $t_0 = -0.99$, $K = 0.34$; combined sexes). Both species exhibited peak hepatosomatic indices (HSI) during the austral summer. Gonadosomatic indices (GSI) peaked during the austral winter for Eastern Fiddler Rays and during the austral spring for Sydney Skates.

Objective: Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is characterized by progressive muscle degeneration proceeding from proximal to distal muscle groups. Although hand grip strength has been evaluated in non-ambulatory individuals with DMD, its relationship with overall functional capacity remains understudied. This study aimed to evaluate the associations between hand grip strength and validated functional measures in non-ambulatory children with DMD. Methods: Hand grip strength was measured using a hand-held dynamometer in 38 non-ambulatory children with DMD (mean age: $12.02 \pm 1.99$ years). Participants were evaluated using the Turkish version of the Egen Klassifikation Scale Version 2 (EK2) for global functional capacity, the Performance of the Upper Limb (PUL) module for upper limb function, and the ABILHAND-Kids questionnaire for manual ability. Results: Dominant hand grip strength was significantly higher than non-dominant hand grip strength ($p < 0.05$). Mean functional scores were $13.02 \pm 5.50$ for EK2, $49.86 \pm 14.34$ for PUL, and $26.81 \pm 7.59$ for ABILHAND-Kids. Hand grip strength demonstrated a statistically significant correlation with EK2 scores ($p < 0.05$). Conclusions: Assessing physical function in severely weakened children with DMD presents significant clinical challenges. Despite demonstrating a moderate correlation, hand grip strength serves as a practical, objective assessment tool for rapidly estimating global functional capacity in non-ambulatory pediatric DMD patients.

### en-058
*Bartonella* species are emerging zoonotic pathogens primarily targeting mammalian erythrocytes and endothelial cells. While bats serve as natural reservoirs for various zoonotic agents, the specific contributions of bats and ectoparasitic bat flies to *Bartonella* transmission cycles require further clarification. In this study, 29 blood DNA samples collected from five bat species on São Luís Island, Maranhão State, northeastern Brazil, were screened for *Bartonella* using a quantitative PCR (qPCR) assay targeting the *nuoG* gene; all blood samples tested negative. Conversely, three out of 15 DNA samples (20%) extracted from streblid bat flies (family Streblidae) were positive for *Bartonella*. BLASTn analysis revealed that the obtained *gltA* and *rpoB* sequences shared 97.2% to 100% identity with *Bartonella* strains previously isolated from bats and bat flies in Brazil and Costa Rica. Bayesian phylogenetic inferences confirmed these sequence alignments, demonstrating that distinct *Bartonella* genotypes circulate within bat fly vectors and expanding our understanding of vector-borne *Bartonella* diversity in South America.
