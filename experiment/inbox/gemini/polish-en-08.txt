Anthropogenic activities have long impacted the Earth's surface, accelerating in recent decades due to land-use changes that drive greenhouse gas emissions, particularly carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$). This study evaluated land-use transitions and associated net $\text{CO}_2$ emissions within a tropical watershed. Land-use maps were generated for 2007, 2010, 2013, and 2016, and transition-driven carbon flux estimates were computed. Net $\text{CO}_2$ calculations integrated spatial transition matrices, historical vegetation records, and pedological data using remote sensing and geoprocessing techniques. The forest cover category exhibited the highest conversion rate to alternative land uses, incurring a net loss of 208.86 ha between 2010 and 2013. ### en-072
Guava rootstock propagation via minicuttings requires high relative humidity followed by an acclimatization period. Plant growth regulators can optimize acclimatization, with brassinosteroids demonstrating positive effects in various fruit crops. This study aimed to shorten the propagation period of the 'BRS Guaraçá' hybrid rootstock by applying Biobras-16 (BB-16) during the acclimatization of minicutting-derived plantlets. The experiment followed a randomized block design with a split-plot arrangement ($5 \times 2$), evaluating five BB-16 concentrations (0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, and 1.0 mg L$^{-1}$) across four replications (five plantlets per plot) during two seasons (winter and spring/summer). After 60 days of acclimatization, shoot and root growth parameters increased significantly in response to BB-16, with optimal performance observed between 0.3 and 0.6 mg L$^{-1}$. Applying BB-16 promotes the growth of 'BRS Guaraçá' minicuttings, accelerating nursery production schedules while enhancing plantlet quality.

### en-073
Background: The introduction of anti-TNF agents represented a major milestone in managing Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), offering improved efficacy and safety over conventional therapies. However, access to biological agents remains challenging in Latin America. Objective: This review summarized current evidence regarding the uptake and penetration rates of biological agents for CD and UC across Latin America. Methods: Data were extracted from a comprehensive systematic review on inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) characteristics in Latin America. Studies detailing the percentage of biological agent use within cohorts from Latin American and Caribbean countries were compiled to analyze regional utilization patterns. Results: Of 61 studies evaluated in the systematic review, 19 provided specific data on the proportion of patients receiving biological therapies. Anti-TNF penetration in CD ranged from 1.51% in Mexico to 46.9% in Colombia, with most cohorts reporting usage rates between 20% and 40%. Conversely, biological therapy utilization was lower in UC, ranging from 0% (2009) to 16.2% (2018) across two Mexican studies. Only two studies reported overall IBD biologic penetration: 13.4% in Colombia and 37.93% in Brazil. No studies documented utilization rates for newer biological agents, such as vedolizumab or ustekinumab. Conclusion: Anti-TNF penetration in Latin America for CD is comparable to global rates but remains low for UC. Given the rising incidence and prevalence of IBD, strategies to expand patient access to anti-TNF agents in UC and newer biological classes are warranted.

### en-076
Spatiotemporal environmental heterogeneity in stream networks drives resource availability, influencing fish feeding ecology. We evaluated the effects of hydrological periods (dry vs. flood) and stream order on the diet of the flag tetra (*Hyphessobrycon heterorhabdus*). Stomach contents were analyzed from 160 individuals sampled across eight 1st- to 3rd-order streams within a preserved basin in the Eastern Amazon. *Hyphessobrycon heterorhabdus* exhibited an omnivorous diet with an insectivorous inclination. During the dry period, the species consumed a higher proportion of autochthonous than allochthonous items. Allochthonous resource intake was highest in 1st- and 2nd-order streams during the dry period and in 1st- and 3rd-order streams during the flood period. These variations highlight how temporal and longitudinal stream gradients interact to dictate food availability, a dynamic sustained by intact riparian corridors providing direct and indirect food inputs.

This study examines how macroeconomic conditions influence accounting information quality by evaluating corporate earnings management behavior across business cycles. Specifically, we investigate: 1) variations in accrual-based earnings management across business cycle phases; and 2) the trade-off between accrual-based and real earnings management. The study sample comprised 247 non-financial firms per year listed on the BM&FBovespa from 2000 to 2015 ($N = 2,501$ firm-year observations). Macroeconomic environments were classified into four business cycle phases based on Schumpeter’s framework: expansion, recession, contraction, and recovery. Discretionary accruals were estimated using the Pae (2005) and Paulo (2007) models, while real earnings management was quantified via Roychowdhury’s (2006) model focusing on abnormal production costs and discretionary operational expenditures. The results demonstrate that earnings management choices—both accrual-based and real manipulation—are significantly shaped by macroeconomic conditions. Managers exhibit distinct opportunistic behavior across economic phases: increasing discretionary accruals during contractionary phases, reducing them during recoveries, and managing earnings downward via real operational manipulation during recessions and contractions.
