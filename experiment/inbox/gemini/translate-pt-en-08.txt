The article aims to analyze the contents and material aspects of the booklet "Viver é lutar" ("To live is to fight"), published in 1963, which was part of the educational collection of the same name created by the Movement for Basic Education (MEB) to support adult literacy classes in the context of Brazilian radio schools in the early 1960s, taking into account the political context of the Brazilian dictatorial regime (1964-1985) and the repression of social movements focused on adult education. The teaching material reveals an effort to introduce elements of backlands popular culture as a didactic-pedagogical strategy to generate identification with the peasants served by the movement, while also considering the different intellectual and ideological trends competing within the Catholic Church mobilized in the construction of the material, related to its political, pedagogical, and religious conceptions, which resulted in a unique model of peasant education. ### tr-072
Remote sensing can be used to estimate meteorological data. Using images of atmospheric variables obtained by the AIRS (Atmospheric Infrared Sounder) sensor aboard the Aqua satellite, this study aimed to estimate the daily maximum, minimum, and mean air temperatures in the region of the Federal District, Brazil. Simple and multiple linear regression models were generated using AIRS sensor data on surface temperature, relative humidity, and atmospheric water vapor as explanatory variables, obtained at a twelve-hour temporal scale (daytime and nighttime) from January 2012 to December 2016. Observed air temperature values from two weather stations in the studied region were used as dependent variables. The fits presented R² = 0.62 and RMSE = 1.64 °C for maximum temperature data, R² = 0.59 and RMSE = 1.50 °C for minimum temperature data, and R² = 0.76 and RMSE = 1.45 °C for mean temperature data. It is concluded that AIRS sensor data are suitable for monitoring air temperature in the Federal District region.

### tr-073
Yeasts have shown good results in the biodegradation of dyes, becoming an environmentally safe and lower-cost alternative for the treatment of effluents containing industrial dyes. Thus, the objective of this work was to study the decolorization of the azo dye Reactive Black 5 (RB5) by the yeast Pichia kudriavzevii SD5. To optimize the decolorization parameters of Reactive Black 5, two experimental designs using a 23 Central Composite Rotatable Design (CCRD) were performed, with each design consisting of 17 runs at 150 rpm for 24 h. The variables studied were pH, temperature, and dye concentration, with decolorization percentage as the response variable. Toxicity tests of the supernatant were also performed after 16 h and 24 h of cultivation, using lettuce (Lactuca sativa) seeds and the microcrustacean Artemia salina. The results showed that P. kudriavzevii SD5 is a yeast tolerant to various types of stress, as it demonstrated the ability to degrade high concentrations of RB5 dye at 45 °C. However, only temperature showed a statistically significant influence (p < 0.05) on the decolorization of RB5. Toxicity bioassays demonstrated a reduction in toxicity after 24 h of cultivation, and the absorbance profile of the supernatant pointed to a degradative decolorization mechanism.

More than half of new HIV infections in 2018 occurred among key populations, including gay men and other Men who have Sex with Men (MSM). Stigma can lead to a series of behavioral consequences, negatively impacting individuals' lives, and can manifest as barriers to accessing public health services. The objective of this article is to analyze the main barriers to accessing HIV testing at a Guidance and Counseling Center in Curitiba and the engagement of gay men and other MSM in health services for early treatment. Primary data collection was conducted following a qualitative methodology, triangulating field observation, document analysis, and interview techniques. The objective of the study was to evaluate the floristic and structural composition in a stretch of secondary forest in the municipality of Dona Francisca - RS. Fifty permanent plots measuring 10 x 20 m were systematically installed, spaced 10 m apart from each other and 40 m between transects, totaling 1 ha. All individuals with CBH ≥ 15.7 cm were sampled, and phytosociological estimators were calculated. A species accumulation curve was performed to check sampling adequacy. For diversity and dominance calculations, the Shannon Index (H') and Pielou's Evenness (J') were used. For potential richness, Jackknife non-parametric estimators were applied. Floristic and structural composition was evaluated using Detrended Correspondence Analysis (DCA). The DBH and height distributions of the tree community were also evaluated, as well as the DBH class distribution for species with an importance value above 5%. Statistical analyses were performed using R software. A total of 65 tree and shrub species with 2,045 individuals distributed across 31 botanical families were identified. The species with an importance value above 5% were Casearia sylvestris (14.04%), Nectandra lanceolata (12.86%), Cupania vernalis (9.57%), Guarea macrophylla (7.29%), and Ocotea puberula (5.07%). Values for diversity and dominance were 2.91 nats.ind-1 and 0.69, respectively, indicating that the area presents low diversity and dominance by a few species. The DCA indicates that the plots are homogeneously distributed within the forest stretch. The diameter distribution followed the expected pattern for the tree community with an inverted J-curve trend, and most trees presented heights concentrated in the intermediate forest stratum. Of the five analyzed species, only Nectandra lanceolata and Ocotea puberula did not display a negative exponential curve. A predominance of individuals in the first DBH and height classes was observed, characterizing the area's resilience following disturbance. Furthermore, it indicates species fluctuations and stability during ecological succession in a forest at an intermediate stage of regeneration.

### tr-079
This article presents the results of a study on hybrid linguistic norms in translated New York Times news articles published in Portuguese on the UOL website. According to Faraco (2008) and Bagno (2012), there is a distinction between the standard norm and the educated norm, the former comprising rules that prescribe linguistic forms according to an idealized model of language that is difficult for most users to follow; and the latter comprising the set of linguistic forms that are actually employed by many writers, being thus more intuitively accessible, although not accepted by the conservative grammatical tradition represented by the standard norm commonly disseminated in grammar books and style manuals. We aim to evaluate whether it is possible to assert that journalistic texts translated from English, similarly to what has already been proven to occur with journalistic texts originally written in Portuguese, are equally permeable to linguistic forms not endorsed by the standard norm.

From the latter, I deduce a function of opacity and invisibility management that necropolitics exercises in conjunction with biopolitics.
