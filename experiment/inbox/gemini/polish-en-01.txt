### en-001
Objective: To analyze the association between depression and various health behaviors, and to assess whether these associations differ by sex or income. Methods: This cross-sectional study analyzed data from 65,803 Brazilian adults (aged 18–59 years) surveyed in the 2019 National Health Survey. The presence or absence of depression was evaluated using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9). Prevalence estimates for smoking, alcohol consumption, physical activity, sedentary behavior, and dietary indicators were calculated according to depression status. Stratified analyses were performed by sex and income, with prevalence ratios estimated using Poisson regression. Results: Significant associations were found between depression and all investigated indicators, except for occasional alcohol consumption. Depression was associated with heavy episodic drinking and insufficient fruit and vegetable intake only among women. In men, the associations of depression with a sedentary lifestyle and being a former smoker were stronger than in women. Occasional alcohol consumption was more prevalent only among men without depression. Income-stratified analysis revealed that the association between depression and physical inactivity was stronger in the higher-income group, whereas its association with heavy episodic drinking was significant only in the lower-income group. Conclusion: These findings highlight the need to incorporate mental health into programs aimed at reducing harmful health behaviors, while accounting for sociodemographic specificities.

### en-002
The management and maintenance of native species under laboratory conditions represent the primary obstacle to proposing their use in bioassays. This study presents results on the controlled management of *Paratanytarsus grimmii* (Diptera: Chironomidae), a parthenogenetic species with a wide geographical distribution. Egg masses were collected from a pre-Andean river in the Araucanía region (39° S), and optimal conditions were established for larval rearing on artificial food, adult handling, egg hatching synchronization, and post-hatch larval retrieval. The *P. grimmii* life cycle included a larval phase (instars I to IV) lasting 23 days to reach the pupal stage; adults laid eggs within the subsequent 48 hours, which then hatched at 72 hours. Laboratory conditions were standardized for incubation room temperature, water pH, artificial diet, photoperiod, and handling techniques for larvae and eggs. Based on these results, the complete life cycle was successfully maintained in the laboratory, supporting the potential application of this species as biological material for toxicity bioassays.

A total of 19,317, 43,874, and 34,460 specimens were registered at the Porto Seguro, Salvador, and Vitória da Conquista units, respectively. The taxonomic distribution of the overall total comprised 80,948 birds (82.90%), 12,007 reptiles (12.30%), 4,661 mammals (4.77%), and 35 arachnids (0.03%). Admissions to CETAS resulted primarily from seizures (67,974; 69.67%), followed by voluntary surrenders (13,367; 13.69%), rescues (12,803; 13.11%), and transfers (2,735; 2.67%). The animals originated from 236 municipalities across the state of Bahia, notably Salvador, Vitória da Conquista, Feira de Santana, Lençóis, Jequié, and Paulo Afonso. The spatial distribution of quantitative seizures indicates that highway BR-116 serves as one of the most prominent routes for illegal wildlife trafficking in Bahia and Brazil.

### en-004
A prosocial lie is a false statement intended to help rather than harm another person. This study investigated potential age and cultural effects on prosocial lying among 97 Brazilian children aged 7 to 11 years, using a task adapted from a previous study conducted with Canadian and Chinese children. The task presented four moral dilemmas followed by questions examining whether children should tell the truth or lie to protect themselves, a friend, or a group. Age had no significant effect on lying scores; however, lying-for-friend scores were significantly higher than lying-for-self and lying-for-group scores. This pattern suggests that, unlike Chinese and Canadian children, Brazilian children consider lying to protect a friend more justifiable than lying to protect self-interests or collective interests.

### en-006
This study aimed to analyze the economic impact of adopting optimized, nutritionally balanced diets among Brazilian families, taking into account the national dietary guidelines and population income disparities. Data were obtained from the 2008–2009 Brazilian Household Budget Survey (550 strata; 55,970 households). Approximately 1,700 food and beverage items purchased by Brazilians were categorized into four groups using the NOVA classification system. Linear programming models were used to estimate isoenergetic diets, using the baseline diet as a benchmark and progressively optimizing healthier diets aligned with the "golden rule" of the dietary guidelines for Brazil. These models incorporated international nutritional recommendations for macro- and micronutrients as well as food acceptance boundaries (10th and 90th percentiles of the population's per capita caloric distribution). Diet cost was calculated by summing the average costs of each food group for both baseline and optimized diets (in BRL per 2,000 kcal/person/day). The economic impact on household budgets was evaluated by comparing the costs of optimized diets with the baseline diet, overall and by income tier. Three healthier diets were successfully optimized. The baseline diet cost was BRL 3.37, varying between low- and high-income strata (BRL 2.62 and BRL 4.17, respectively). Across all income levels, diet costs decreased as alignment with the guidelines increased. However, low-income households committed more than twice the proportion of their budget to diet costs compared to high-income households (20.2% vs. 7.96%, respectively). Consequently, healthier dietary patterns can be adopted within existing or reduced household food budgets.

### en-007
This study aimed to evaluate the antioxidant responses of sugarcane (*Saccharum* spp.) varieties exposed to drought, salinity, and combined drought + salinity stress. Micropropagated seedlings of six sugarcane varieties were subjected to drought, salinity, or combined drought + salinity stress. The experiment was conducted in May 2012 in a greenhouse at the Universidade Federal Rural de Pernambuco (UFRPE) using a randomized 6 × 4 factorial design (six varieties × four treatments) with four replicates. Lipid peroxidation, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) content, and relative water content (RWC) were evaluated. Antioxidant defense system responses were assessed by measuring superoxide dismutase (SOD), ascorbate peroxidase (APX), and catalase (CAT) activities. Combined drought + salinity stress induced higher levels of lipid peroxidation and/or elevated H2O2 concentrations across the sugarcane varieties. Antioxidant enzyme responses to individual drought and salinity stresses varied depending on the variety. Under combined stress conditions, enzyme activity appeared to be inhibited, indicating that the physiological response to combined stress differs from the additive effects of single stresses. High malondialdehyde (MDA) concentrations combined with low RWC may serve as effective indicators of multiple-stress sensitivity in sugarcane. Varieties RB99395 and RB867515 exhibited more efficient stress responses, maintaining cellular water content under both drought and salinity conditions.

To better understand the variables influencing choices among Psychology specializations, this study adapted the Scale of Interests by Areas of Psychology (EIAPsi) for the Portuguese context, evaluated its internal structural validity, and examined its relationship with personality traits. The sample comprised 340 Portuguese university students who completed the Portuguese version of the EIAPsi and the NEO Five-Factor Inventory (NEO-FFI). Openness to experience and agreeableness were the personality dimensions most frequently correlated with interest in specific areas of Psychology. ### en-009
This study aimed to evaluate the vertical transmission of *Neospora caninum* in naturally infected ewes and to track antibody kinetics in their offspring. Forty-eight pregnant ewes from five flocks were assigned to two groups: G1 (seropositive for anti-*N. caninum* antibodies, n = 19) and G2 (seronegative, n = 29). Blood samples were collected from ewes and their lambs immediately at birth (pre-colostral), and at 2, 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, and 56 days post-partum. Serum antibody levels were determined using an indirect immunofluorescence assay. Of the 19 seropositive ewes, six (31.6%) gave birth to pre-colostrally seropositive lambs, all of which remained seropositive throughout the 56-day evaluation period. Only one lamb, born to a ewe with an antibody titer of 200, seroconverted following colostrum ingestion. All lambs born to seronegative ewes remained seronegative throughout the study. These results indicate that transplacental transmission is an important route for *N. caninum* dissemination in sheep flocks and that seropositive lambs maintain circulating antibodies during the early post-natal period.

This pilot case study had a dual objective: to examine power dynamics and voice within small-group interactions in a secondary classroom, and to reflect on the implications of researching one's own pedagogical practice as a teacher-researcher. Grounded in Bakhtin’s theory of dialogism, audio recordings of small-group interactions were compared with students' individual written work to determine how different voices were expressed and utilized. Four major themes emerged: power and authority, positions of submission, silence, and the voice of the text. The findings demonstrate that real-time classroom instruction can obscure subtle identity and power dynamics; however, reflective research provides the temporal distance necessary for educators to evaluate their instructional strategies objectively and construct new pedagogical insights.
