### en-061
Nuclear techniques based on gamma-ray attenuation are widely used in industrial applications to measure flow rates, determine fluid density, predict inorganic scaling in oil pipelines, and evaluate industrial mixing processes. Implementing these techniques effectively requires detailed evaluations of radiation source parameters and detector geometries, such as solid angle and detection efficiency. This study aimed to calculate the geometric solid angle and the effective solid angle subtended by a NaI(Tl) scintillation detector. The effective solid angle accounts for attenuation in the intermediate medium and additional radiation-matter interaction effects. Mathematical models were developed using the MCNP6 Monte Carlo code to evaluate the proposed measurement geometry. Point isotropic sources ($^{241}\text{Am}$ and $^{137}\text{Cs}$) were positioned at multiple locations relative to the NaI(Tl) detector to evaluate frontal and lateral solid angle contributions, which are critical for determining the intrinsic efficiency response function. The results demonstrated that the discrepancy between the geometric solid angle and the effective solid angle reached 20.17% for $^{241}\text{Am}$ and 2.58% for $^{137}\text{Cs}$, indicating that incorporating the effective solid angle is essential for accurate calculations.

This paper analyzes the role of industrial policy in promoting technological upgrading in Brazil. ### en-064
This study evaluates the influence of characteristic compressive strength ($f_{ck}$), cross-sectional geometry, and eccentric axial loads on the ultimate strength of square, cruciform, T-shaped, and L-shaped reinforced concrete sections subjected to oblique composite bending. A computational algorithm was developed to calculate bending strength interaction diagrams, incorporating the idealized parabola-rectangle stress-strain relationships specified in NBR 6118 for concrete grades ranging from $f_{ck} = 20\text{ to }90\text{ MPa}$. The results demonstrate that the influence of $f_{ck}$ is more pronounced under high axial loads and that the geometry of the failure surface in interaction diagrams depends directly on $f_{ck}$ and longitudinal rebar distribution. Under low compressive axial loads, higher oblique composite bending strengths are achieved by increasing the reinforcement area in tensile zones. Conversely, as compressive axial forces increase, increasing the reinforcement ratio and expanding the concrete area in compression zones yield higher bending moment capacities.

### en-066
A new species of *Parotocinclus*, apparently endemic to the Rio Jacuípe basin in Bahia State, Brazil, is described. The species is distinguished from all congeners except *P. bidentatus*, *P. cabessadecuia*, *P. dani*, *P. halbothi*, *P. muriaensis*, *P. pentakelis*, *P. seridoensis*, and *P. spilurus* by possessing a reduced adipose fin. ### en-067
This study evaluated the effects of varying spray volumes and fungicide rates on Asian soybean rust (*Phakopsora pachyrhizi*) control. Field and laboratory experiments were conducted between 2014 and 2016. Field treatments evaluated combinations of fungicide application rates (45 + 52.5, 60 + 70, and 75 + 87.5 g a.i. ha$^{-1}$ for trifloxystrobin + prothioconazole; 45 + 22.5, 60 + 30, and 75 + 37.5 g a.i. ha$^{-1}$ for azoxystrobin + benzovindiflupyr) across three spray volumes (100, 150, and 200 L ha$^{-1}$). A spray volume of 200 L ha$^{-1}$ significantly improved disease control only during the third field application (control efficacy: 68.4%–70.1%). However, grain yields did not differ significantly among spray volumes at label rates (3,422, 3,526, and 3,601 kg ha$^{-1}$) or elevated rates (3,529, 3,586, and 3,550 kg ha$^{-1}$). Reducing fungicide rates significantly decreased disease control across experiments (20%–39% reduction in field trials; up to 71.4% reduction in laboratory trials).

### en-069
Aim: To evaluate the combined impact of muscular temporomandibular disorders (TMD) and malocclusion on oral health-related quality of life, masticatory capacity, emotional stress, and pain parameters in young adults. Methods: This cross-sectional study evaluated four groups ($n = 15$ per group): G1 (healthy controls without TMD or malocclusion), G2 (muscular TMD and malocclusion), G3 (muscular TMD without malocclusion), and G4 (malocclusion without TMD). Muscular TMD was diagnosed using the RDC/TMD protocol. Assessments included oral health-related quality of life (OHIP-14), masticatory performance ($X_{50}$ median particle size test), perceived stress (PSS-14), depression (MDI), pain intensity, and salivary cortisol levels. Group comparisons were conducted using one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc tests ($X_{50}$, stress, cortisol) and generalized estimating equations via the GENMOD procedure with Wald tests (OHIP-14, pain scores). Association patterns were evaluated using Pearson's correlation and Fisher's exact tests. Results: Co-occurring muscular TMD and malocclusion significantly impaired masticatory performance ($p < 0.05$). Groups with TMD, regardless of occlusal status, demonstrated significantly lower OHIP-14 scores and elevated psychological and salivary cortisol stress measures ($p < 0.05$). Depressive symptoms did not differ significantly among groups. Quality of life, stress measures, and pain perception were significantly correlated. Conclusion: Muscular TMD interacting with malocclusion exacerbates masticatory performance deficits. Overall, TMD exerts a stronger negative impact on quality of life and physiological stress, whereas malocclusion primarily impairs masticatory function.
