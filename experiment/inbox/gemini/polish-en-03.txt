Aligning mission statements with strategic planning provides clear communication to stakeholders regarding long-term SGP objectives, based on the premise that transparent and objective communication reduces management failure risks. ### en-022
Cover crop residue can induce changes in soil microbial activity and population dynamics, impacting carbon (C) and nitrogen (N) cycling and providing key insights for sustainable land-use planning in the Brazilian Cerrado. This study evaluated the effects of cover crop straw on soil microbial attributes and organic matter quality. Experimental units consisted of 100 g of sieved soil placed in plastic cups and mixed with cover crop residue. Treatments were arranged in a completely randomized design (CRD) in a 7 × 7 + 1 factorial scheme, incorporating seven types of straw evaluated at 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, 63, and 105 days after incubation, alongside an unamended control, with three replicates. The metabolic quotient ($q\text{CO}_2$) ranged from 0.1% (*Crotalaria spectabilis* at 14 days) to 5.1% (*Cajanus cajan* ‘IAPAR 43’ at 42 days). Organic carbon and nitrogen reservoirs were differentially affected by legume and grass residues over time. Incorporating *Brachiaria brizantha* and *Mucuna aterrima* positively influenced microbial attributes ($q\text{CO}_2$, microbial biomass C, and microbial biomass N) up to day 21 of incubation, exhibiting low $\text{CO}_2$ loss and increased microbial biomass. All evaluated species showed potential for inclusion in crop rotation systems to support no-tillage practices in the Cerrado of Piauí; however, species selection should be tailored to specific management goals. Overall, using cover crop residues serves as an effective strategy for enhancing the biological quality of Cerrado soils in northeastern Brazil.

### en-024
Given the expanding scope of Thysanoptera research in Brazil, this study aimed to characterize thrips diversity in historically understudied regions. We surveyed thrips species (Insecta: Thysanoptera) in a *restinga* coastal vegetation fragment at the State University of Ceará (UECE), Fortaleza, Ceará State, and calculated estimated richness and diversity indices. Field sampling was conducted from 2011 to 2013 across flowers of 86 plant species. Samples were analyzed at the Laboratory of Insect-Plant Interactions using stereomicroscopy. We collected 456 adults and 58 immatures representing 14 identified species, along with an unidentified *Treherniella* species. *Microcephalothrips abdominalis* was recorded across numerous host plants, while *Frankliniella insularis* was the most abundant species. Approximately two-thirds of the total species richness was associated with three plant families (Amaranthaceae, Caesalpiniaceae, and Poaceae); six thrips species were host-specific to a single plant species. Observed species richness closely matched estimations derived from Bootstrap and Jackknife 1 estimators. The Shannon-Wiener ($H'$) and Simpson ($D$) diversity indices were 1.7607 and 0.7769, respectively. Although the identified thrips species are common, we established 46 new plant–thrips associations, 13 of which represent true host interactions, highlighting the ecological role of coastal *restinga* vegetation in sustaining thrips populations.

### en-025
This paper explores the implications of a post-humanist framework for physical disability by conceptualizing the use of assistive technologies (AT) by disabled individuals as the emergence of a low-tech cyborg. Drawing on examples of communication ATs, we draw analogies between ATs and language in shaping selfhood and social contexts. Because ATs carry ideological assumptions, they can be framed either as mechanisms to "repair" an "impaired" body or as strategies to challenge physical and social environments that render individuals disabled while privileged others are deemed "able-bodied." We argue that adopting a low-tech cyborg identity promotes social inclusion when disability is recognized as a structural product of the environment rather than an intrinsic individual dysfunction. Under this premise, we outline two inclusion strategies for the low-tech cyborg: disembodiment of the self and embodied virtuality. However, we note that low-tech cyborg identities emerge through either necessity or choice, and that systemic socioeconomic factors driving general inequality also shape the social inclusion or exclusion of low-tech cyborgs. ATs can be adopted or modified by choice, potentially widening the divide between cyborg "haves" and "have-nots," while both groups remain susceptible to cognitive and affective extraction within cognitive capitalism. We conclude that the value of a post-humanist perspective on disability lies not in standardizing "impaired humans" nor in augmenting "able-bodied humans," but in maintaining ethical connectivity among selves through virtual embodiment or embodied virtuality.

### en-026
Designing water distribution systems primarily involves selecting pipe diameters that satisfy hydraulic requirements while minimizing capital and operational costs. Among existing methods for economic pipe diameter selection, Bresse’s equation remains widely used by design engineers. This study evaluated the performance of Bresse’s equation, the Modified Bresse-Polak-Wightwick (MBPW) method, and the Modified Linear Velocity Pipe Cost (MLVPC) method, comparing them against the Modified Ratio Economic Cost (MREC) methodology. Based on our findings, we advise designers against using the MBPW and MLVPC methods. For small-scale water distribution systems, Bresse’s equation remains viable, provided an appropriate coefficient $k$ is applied. For high-density polyethylene (HDPE) pipes, we propose $k$ values of 1.17 to 1.18. For PVC DEFOFO and glass-fiber reinforced plastic (GFRP) pipes, we recommend ranges of 1.19–1.23 and 1.29–1.32, respectively. For larger-scale water distribution networks, we recommend adopting the MREC methodology, as calibrated $k$ coefficients for Bresse’s equation cannot be reliably established at higher dimensions.

Research on aquatic decomposition remains limited in subtropical South America. Most studies were conducted in lotic systems (streams: 39.6%) and focused primarily on breakdown dynamics and associated macroinvertebrate communities (42.5%). A substantial research gap persists for lentic ecosystems (e.g., lakes: 10%). Conclusion: Studies on aquatic organic matter decomposition in subtropical South America are still emerging. While the ecological role of shredders is relatively well documented, the contribution of microbial decomposers requires further evaluation. Furthermore, our analysis indicates that international scientific collaboration significantly enhanced publication output on this topic.

### en-029
This study evaluated the reading time of street names displayed on dynamic, egocentrically referenced maps in In-Car Route Guidance and Navigation Systems (RGNS). We assessed two road network configurations (grid-like vs. non-grid-like) during street-name search tasks across two route locations: the vehicle's current segment and upcoming maneuver points. In non-grid-like road networks, when drivers searched for streets at upcoming maneuver points, labels aligned horizontally and adjacent to the route were read significantly faster than labels placed directly along the route line. Obliquely oriented labels required longer reading times than horizontally aligned labels. Dynamic egocentric navigation maps should display street labels horizontally aligned and positioned adjacent to the route line at decision points across both grid and non-grid road networks. Furthermore, street names should be positioned near the vehicle position symbol to optimize readability.

The Annonaceae family exhibits high anatomical and structural uniformity across species. Chemotaxonomic profiling is widely used to resolve chemical patterns within plant families across phylogenetic levels. This study aimed to construct a comprehensive dataset of isolated secondary metabolites from the Annonaceae family and perform chemotaxonomic evaluations using self-organizing maps (SOMs). The dataset compiled 5,321 botanical occurrences corresponding to 1,860 unique chemical structures across all Annonaceae subfamilies. Diterpenes represented 366 unique compounds across 533 botanical occurrences reported in both Annonoideae and Malmeoideae subfamilies. The tribes Annoneae, Xylopieae, and Miliuseae contained the highest concentration of botanical records and were selected for computational modeling. Molecular descriptors derived from diterpene structures and their botanical occurrence data were used to generate SOMs. The resulting SOM models successfully discriminated between the selected tribes, achieving a classification accuracy exceeding 70%. These findings align with established morphological and molecular phylogenies. The developed SOM models provide a predictive framework for determining the phylogenetic distribution of diterpenes, accelerating secondary metabolite discovery and bioactivity screening in Annonaceae.
