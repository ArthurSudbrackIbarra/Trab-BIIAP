The largest volume of grape juice produced in Brazil comes from the Isabel cultivar. However, blends with other cultivars are necessary to improve juice quality, which increases demand for other cultivars, especially Bordô. Therefore, the objective of this study was to evaluate the influence of the cultivars BRS Rúbea, BRS Cora, BRS Violeta, BRS Carmem, BRS Magna, and Bordô in blends with Isabel regarding the physicochemical, colorimetric, and sensory characteristics of juices produced in the Vale do Rio do Peixe region - SC. The juices were prepared at Epagri - Videira Experimental Station - SC, as follows: (ISA) 100% Isabel; (ISA+RUB) 70% Isabel + 30% Rúbea; (ISA+COR) 70% Isabel + 30% Cora; (ISA+VIO) 70% Isabel + 30% Violeta; (ISA+CAR) 70% Isabel + 30% Carmem; (ISA+MAG) 70% Isabel + 30% Magna; (ISA+BOR) 70% Isabel + 30% Bordô, and evaluated for physicochemical composition, colorimetric attributes, and sensory profile. The highest titratable acidity was verified in ISA+MAG, followed by ISA+BOR. The ISA+VIO juices obtained the highest mean soluble solids content, not differing only from ISA+CAR, which also presented the lowest acidity among treatments. The ISA+VIO and ISA+CAR juices exhibited higher soluble solids content compared to 100% Isabel juice. The lowest lightness was observed in ISA+VIO juices, followed by ISA+CAR. Regarding sensory attributes, significant differences were observed only for color and acidity. The ISA+VIO, ISA+CAR, and ISA+BOR juices received the highest scores for the color attribute. All tested cultivars influenced the colorimetric characteristics of the juice, proving to be alternatives for improving the color of Isabel juices. Sensorially, only color and acidity were influenced by the different blends.

The objective of this article is to verify the factors associated with seeking preventive health services among the Brazilian adult population. The sample consisted of participants from the National Health Survey (2013) who reported seeking health services in the previous 15 days, classified into "treatment/diagnosis" or "preventive". Exploratory variables included sex, age group, race/skin color, marital status, educational level, household location, private health plan coverage, and time enrolled in the Family Health Strategy (FHS). Associations were verified by prevalence ratios (PR) obtained through Poisson regression models, accounting for the complex sample design. The sample comprised 32,377 individuals who sought health services, of which 12.94% sought "preventive" actions. Seeking preventive care was more frequent among women and less frequent among older individuals, those not living with a partner, and those with lower educational attainment. Lacking private health insurance was associated with lower preventive care seeking. Being enrolled in the FHS was not associated with seeking preventive actions. Despite efforts and recognition of the importance of prevention, most individuals still seek health services for treatment.

The incidence of degradation agents on buildings is responsible for triggering degradation mechanisms that lead to the development of anomalies, which reduce the performance of building systems. In this context, investigating the relationship between anomalies, agents, and mechanisms is essential to provide inputs for service life studies and the development of more durable materials and elements. The objective of this study is to investigate the severity and extent of degradation of mortar-rendered facades, identifying patterns that may condition the degradation of this element. This approach is based on inspecting buildings in use using the Degradation Measurement Method. The methodology is applied to the study of 90 facade samples from buildings aged between 20 and 60 years located in Brasília, Brazil. Analyses are based on degradation indicators quantified from the investigated variables, specifically solar orientation and facade zones. Results allow the identification of typical degradation patterns. Cracking is the anomaly that degrades facades the most. The top zone of the facade exhibits the highest degradation severity. In recent years, there has been a continuous advancement of the oil palm agricultural frontier in the Amazon region, causing changes in land cover in this region. Given the need to understand the influence of this crop on surface albedo, this study aims to compare surface albedo fluctuations in an oil palm crop area with those of pasture, forest, and secondary vegetation. To perform this comparison, orbital data based on the MCD43A3 product from the Terra/MODIS satellite for the years 2015 and 2016 were used. The selected samples for the study area showed that oil palm had little variation, with estimated mean values of α = 0.14, being statistically distinct from other land uses and covers, with albedo ranking as: Pasture > Oil Palm > Secondary Vegetation > Forest. This demonstrates that potential conversions from one cover to another can influence the surface radiation balance; that is, if oil palm cultivation continues advancing over pasture areas, this would decrease surface albedo and consequently alter the microclimate. However, if expansion occurs over Secondary Vegetation or Forest areas, a reversal will occur, which could contribute to climate change.
