### en-011
In this article, residual indicators were used to characterize the quality of numerical solutions to the advection-diffusion-reaction equation in a saturated porous medium. Both high- and low-advection regimes were considered. The low-advection regime was illustrated using a problem with constant data, whereas the high-advection regime involved non-constant data. In the latter case, residual quantities associated with the data must be incorporated into the residual estimates related to spatial approximation, and an auxiliary problem must be solved to obtain accurate temporal estimates. Representing the residual indicators as surfaces over the finite element mesh provides a detailed view of the regions requiring refinement. This representation also makes it possible to infer the contribution of each estimate to the global estimator and to track the evolution of the residual surfaces as the contaminant front advances throughout the simulation. Furthermore, the numerical values of the indicators help identify the elements to be refined, compare the magnitudes of the individual contributions across different meshes, and provide a better understanding of the composition of the global estimates.

### en-012
We present a checklist of Odonata (Insecta) species occurring in a fragment of Seasonal Deciduous Forest within the Atlantic Forest biome in the central region of Rio Grande do Sul (RS), southern Brazil, together with a checklist of odonate species recorded in this state's corresponding phytoecological region. We also provide comments on the seasonal distribution of species found in the study area. Two streams and seven farm ponds located in the middle reaches of the Jacuí River basin were surveyed between December 2007 and February 2009. A total of 49 species belonging to 21 genera and six families were recorded. Argia serva Hagen in Selys, 1865 (Coenagrionidae) was recorded for the first time in the state, increasing the total number of Odonata species known from Rio Grande do Sul to 183. The species recorded in the study area represent approximately 26% of the known Odonata diversity in RS. Libellulidae was the most species-rich family, with 22 species (approximately 45% of the total), followed by Coenagrionidae, with 18 species (37%). The checklist for Seasonal Deciduous Forest in RS indicated the occurrence of 83 Odonata species in this phytoecological region, representing approximately 45% of the species known in the state. This high diversity may be associated with the density of the vegetation structure. In the study area, 20 species were recorded in streams and 45 in farm ponds. Species occurrence exhibited marked seasonal patterns: 88% of the species were recorded between summer and autumn, and no species were detected in streams during winter. Furthermore, 70% of the species recorded in farm ponds occurred in only one or two seasons, whereas 65% of those found in streams occurred exclusively in a single season. These findings indicate that the life cycle of Odonata in southern Brazil is strongly influenced by seasonal temperature patterns.

Objective: To evaluate and correlate cognitive function, balance, and gait in older women with chronic peripheral vestibular dizziness and compare these outcomes with those of older women without vestibular disorders. Methods: Twenty-two women who had experienced episodes of peripheral vestibular dizziness for at least six months participated in the study. Participants were classified according to dizziness severity as having moderate (n = 11) or severe (n = 11) symptoms. The control group comprised 11 women without vestibulopathy, light-headedness, or dizziness. Cognitive function and semi-static and dynamic balance were assessed using the Balance Master (Neurocom International, Inc., Clackamas, OR), while symptom severity was measured using the Dizziness Handicap Inventory. Inferential statistical analyses and correlation analyses were performed to examine relationships among cognitive, balance, and stability variables. Results: Compared with the control group, women with severe dizziness exhibited greater anteroposterior center-of-pressure sway velocity, shorter step lengths, and slower gait. Regarding cognitive variables, the group with severe dizziness showed significant correlations between cognitive performance and stability and gait variables. Conclusion: The relationships among cognitive function, balance, and gait were stronger in women with severe dizziness than in those without vestibulopathy.

### en-016
Experimental measurements of systems characterized by slow gas transport kinetics are generally considered relatively straightforward compared with measurements of very fast systems. However, when transport processes have time constants on the order of several hours, not only the measurements themselves but also the analysis and interpretation of the resulting data present challenges. Under such conditions, accurately determining the process time constant is not a trivial task. In this study, measurements of CO₂ diffusion in Na,Cs-Rho crystals obtained using the zero-length column (ZLC) technique were used as a case study to investigate the application of this technique to very slow adsorption processes. The system, which has a time constant of approximately 8 h, demonstrates the importance of employing the partial-loading approach to determine an unambiguous time constant from the analysis of ZLC desorption curves. The conventional analysis was further refined by applying the nonlinear ZLC model to account for isotherm nonlinearity, which results in concentration-dependent diffusivity. Finally, the method proposed by Cavalcante was applied to confirm the three-dimensional (3-D) diffusion pathway in the system.

### en-019
Data from field experiments conducted in vineyards growing the Festival table grape variety in Petrolina, Pernambuco, Brazil, between October 2009 and November 2010, were used to evaluate the influence of plastic covers on grapevine productivity and economic profitability in the São Francisco River Valley. Three treatments were investigated: an uncovered canopy (UC), a plastic cover positioned 50 cm above the canopy (PC50), and a plastic cover positioned 100 cm above the canopy (PC100). The results indicated that the greater availability of radiative fluxes at berry height under the PC100 treatment contributed to higher productivity, whereas PC50 provided the lowest radiative fluxes at berry height and resulted in substantially lower productivity. The yield under PC100 exceeded that under UC and PC50 by 11 t ha⁻¹ and 12.3 t ha⁻¹, respectively. The PC100 treatment also resulted in the lowest incidence of berry defects, contributing to higher total revenue. In contrast, the markedly lower productivity observed under PC50 resulted in considerable economic losses.
