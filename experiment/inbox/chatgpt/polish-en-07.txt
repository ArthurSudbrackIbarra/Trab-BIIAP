**Aim:** To evaluate the combined impact of muscular temporomandibular disorders (TMD) and malocclusion on quality of life, masticatory capacity, and emotional characteristics in young adults.

**Methods:** This cross-sectional study included four groups (n = 15 each): G1, individuals without TMD or malocclusion; G2, individuals with both TMD and malocclusion; G3, individuals with TMD but no malocclusion; and G4, individuals without TMD but with malocclusion. Muscular TMD was diagnosed using the RDC/TMD criteria. Data were collected on quality of life (OHIP-14), masticatory capacity (X50), perceived stress (PSS-14), depression (MDI), pain intensity, and salivary cortisol. Comparative analyses included one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for X50, stress, and cortisol, and GENMOD followed by the Wald test for OHIP-14 and pain data. Associations were assessed using Fisher’s exact test and Pearson’s correlation analysis.

**Results:** The coexistence of muscular TMD and malocclusion was associated with significantly lower masticatory capacity (p < 0.05). Regardless of occlusal condition, the TMD groups had significantly lower OHIP-14 scores and higher stress levels, both self-reported and hormonal (p < 0.05). No statistically significant differences in depression were observed among the groups. Significant positive correlations were found among quality of life, stress, and pain perception.

**Conclusion:** The coexistence of muscular TMD and malocclusion exacerbates their negative effects on masticatory capacity. Although the impact of this comorbidity varies, TMD has a greater negative effect on quality of life and stress, whereas malocclusion has a greater impact on mastication.
