This study aimed to assess the extent to which the construct of survival, growth, and profitability (SGP) is disclosed in the mission statements of Brazilian companies and in the collective discourse of different economic sectors, classified according to Exame magazine's annual "Biggest & Best" ranking. It seeks to address a gap in the literature by examining the disclosure of the SGP construct in the mission statements of large Brazilian companies. Considering mission statements as the starting point of strategic planning, analyzing the disclosure of the SGP construct makes it possible to discuss the relevance of mission statements to the formulation, implementation, and monitoring of strategic plans. The value of aligning mission statements with strategic planning lies in effectively communicating long-term SGP objectives to stakeholders, based on the premise that comprehensive and objective communication minimizes the risk of failures throughout the management process. Collective Subject Discourse (CSD) was employed to develop a qualification metric for the constituent elements of organizational mission statements. This approach made it possible to identify whether Brazilian companies, grouped by economic sector, explicitly address, obscure, or omit the SGP construct in their mission statements. From an academic perspective, these findings contribute to a better understanding of the constituent elements of mission statements among large Brazilian companies. For organizations, the results highlight the need to critically reflect on the content included when formulating mission statements.

### en-022
Cover crop residues can alter soil microbial activity and population dynamics, with implications for environmental conditions and carbon (C) and nitrogen (N) cycling. Understanding these effects provides important information for planning appropriate land use in the Cerrado. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of cover crop residues on soil microbial attributes and organic matter quality. The experimental units consisted of 100 g of sieved soil placed in small plastic cups and mixed with cover crop residues. Treatments were arranged in a completely randomized design using a 7 × 7 + 1 factorial scheme, comprising seven residue types evaluated at 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, 63, and 105 days after incubation, along with a control treatment without residue, with three replicates. The metabolic quotient (qCO₂) ranged from 0.1% for Crotalaria spectabilis at 14 days to 5.1% for Cajanus cajan 'IAPAR 43' at 42 days. Soil organic carbon and nitrogen pools were affected differently by legume and grass residues across the evaluation periods. Incorporation of Brachiaria brizantha and Mucuna aterrima positively influenced microbial attributes (qCO₂, microbial biomass carbon [Cmic], and microbial biomass nitrogen [Nmic]) up to the 21st day of incubation, resulting in low carbon dioxide losses and increased microbial populations. All species evaluated showed potential for use in crop rotation systems, supporting the adoption of no-tillage practices in the Cerrado region of Piauí. However, species selection should be guided by specific management objectives. Thus, cover crops represent an important strategy for improving the biological quality of soils in the northeastern Brazilian Cerrado.

### en-024
With the growing number of studies on Thysanoptera in Brazil, research has increasingly focused on improving our understanding of thrips diversity in areas where their fauna has historically received little attention. Accordingly, this study investigated the diversity of thrips (Insecta: Thysanoptera) associated with a fragment of restinga vegetation on the campus of the State University of Ceará (UECE), in Fortaleza, Ceará, Brazil, and calculated species richness estimates and diversity indices. Samples were collected from flowers of 86 plant species between 2011 and 2013. The collected material was examined under a stereomicroscope at the Laboratory of Insect–Plant Interactions. A total of 456 adults and 58 immature specimens were collected, representing 14 species and one unidentified species of Treherniella. Microcephalothrips abdominalis was recorded on the largest number of host plants, while Frankliniella insularis was the most abundant species. Approximately two-thirds of the total thrips species richness was associated with three plant families: Amaranthaceae, Caesalpiniaceae, and Poaceae. Six thrips species were each associated with only one plant species. Observed species richness was close to the estimates obtained using Bootstrap and Jackknife 1 analyses. The Shannon–Wiener (H′) and Simpson (D) diversity indices were 1.7607 and 0.7769, respectively. Although the species recorded are common, 46 new associations between plant species and thrips were identified, 13 of which represented true host associations. These findings demonstrate the importance of coastal vegetation in maintaining thrips populations.

### en-025
This paper explores the implications of a posthumanist perspective on physical disability by examining the use of assistive technologies (ATs) by people with disabilities as the introduction of a low-tech cyborg into the world. In this context, we highlight examples of communication-related ATs and draw analogies between ATs and languages in their roles in shaping individual identities and social contexts. Because ATs are ideologically informed, they can be understood either as tools for "fixing" an "impaired" individual or as strategies for overcoming physical and social environments that disable certain people while rendering others "able-bodied." We argue that becoming a low-tech cyborg can constitute a form of social inclusion when disability is understood as a product of contextual conditions rather than an inherent dysfunction of the individual. Based on this premise, we identify two strategies for the social inclusion of the low-tech cyborg: disembodiment of the self and embodied virtuality. We also emphasize that low-tech cyborgs may emerge either out of necessity or by choice, and that the same socioeconomic factors underlying broader forms of inequality also shape their social exclusion or inclusion. Consequently, ATs may be adopted and transformed through individual choice in ways that widen the gap between cyborgs who have access to these technologies and those who do not. At the same time, both groups may become increasingly vulnerable to cognitive and affective exploitation within the context of cognitive capitalism. We conclude that the potential of a posthumanist perspective on disability should lie neither in making "impaired humans" whole nor in making "whole humans" more than human, but rather in keeping individuals ethically connected to others, whether through virtual embodiment or embodied virtuality.

### en-026
The design of water distribution systems primarily involves determining pipe diameters that satisfy system requirements while minimizing the annual costs of installation and operation. Among the methodologies available for determining economically optimal pipe diameters, Bresse's equation remains widely used by designers. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of Bresse's equation, MBPW, and MLVPC by comparing them with MREC. We recommend that designers avoid using MBPW and MLVPC. For small-scale water distribution systems, Bresse's equation can be used, provided that an appropriate value is selected for its coefficient k. For high-density polyethylene (HDPE) pipes, we propose values of k = 1.17 or k = 1.18. For PVC DEFOFO and glass-fiber-reinforced polymer (GFRP) pipes, we recommend ranges of 1.19–1.23 and 1.29–1.32, respectively. For larger water distribution systems, we recommend MREC as the preferred methodology for determining the economically optimal pipe diameter, since no suitable value for the Bresse equation coefficient can be established in these cases.

Research on this topic in subtropical South America remains incipient. Most studies were conducted in streams (39.6%) and focused on decomposition processes and associated invertebrates (42.5%). Finally, our findings demonstrate that scientific collaboration has contributed to increased publication output on this topic.

### en-028
This paper presents PeMSyn, a free software tool designed to support the design and simulation of permanent magnet synchronous machines. PeMSyn enables users to design permanent magnet synchronous machines, develop their winding distributions, and evaluate machine performance. Performance evaluation is performed using finite element analysis (FEA), enabled by the integration of MATLAB and FEMM. This integration allows users to obtain several machine parameters. Another key feature of PeMSyn is its modular architecture, which allows each functionality to operate independently and enables users to add new functionalities and machine topologies, either independently or through future software updates. Developed in MATLAB as a graphical user interface (GUI), PeMSyn is designed to be simple and user-friendly. Furthermore, users can easily translate all interface labels and variable names into their native languages using a language description file, which is already available in English and Portuguese. This paper describes the software modules, the machine design process, the underlying equations, and an application. To provide practical guidance on using PeMSyn, a machine design example is presented, and its performance is evaluated.

### en-029
This study aimed to evaluate the time required to read street names displayed on dynamic, egocentrically referenced maps designed for in-car route guidance and navigation systems. Two types of road network patterns (grid-like and non-grid-like) were considered, and search tasks were designed to locate street names in two different route segments: the segment corresponding to the driver's current position and the segment where the driver would need to maneuver. For non-grid-like road networks, when participants searched for the name of the road on which they would need to maneuver, street names displayed horizontally and adjacent to the route were read significantly faster than those positioned along the route. Street names displayed obliquely required more reading time than those aligned horizontally. These findings indicate that dynamic, egocentrically referenced maps should display street names horizontally and adjacent to the route in the segment where the driver will maneuver, regardless of the road network pattern. Street names should also be positioned next to the vehicle symbol.

The Annonaceae family is characterized by considerable anatomical and structural uniformity. Chemotaxonomy is widely used to identify chemical patterns within plant families at different phylogenetic levels. This study aimed to compile a dataset of all secondary metabolites isolated from the Annonaceae family and conduct a chemotaxonomic analysis using self-organizing maps (SOMs). The dataset comprised 5,321 botanical occurrences and 1,860 unique molecules distributed across all Annonaceae subfamilies. Diterpenes accounted for 366 unique compounds and 533 botanical occurrences, distributed across the Annonoideae and Malmeoideae subfamilies. The tribes Annoneae, Xylopieae, and Miliuseae had the highest numbers of botanical occurrences and were therefore selected for analysis. Molecular descriptors of the diterpenes and their corresponding botanical occurrences were used to generate the SOMs. The resulting maps revealed clear and consistent separation among the tribes, with a match rate exceeding 70%. These findings are consistent with existing morphological and molecular data. The models may be used to predict the phylogenetic placement of specific diterpenes and facilitate research into Annonaceae secondary metabolites and their biological potential.
