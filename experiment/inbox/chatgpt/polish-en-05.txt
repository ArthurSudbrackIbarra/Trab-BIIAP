This study aimed to assess heavy metal concentrations in sweet potatoes (Ipomoea batatas) and spinach grown in the presence of potential contamination sources, including uncontrolled municipal waste, motor vehicle emissions, pesticides, and fertilizers, in the Urban West Region of Zanzibar. Twenty samples were randomly collected from local markets and three farms located along a roadside in the region. The samples were analyzed using energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence at the Tanzania Atomic Energy Commission in Arusha. The essential and toxic elements Mn, Cu, Fe, As, Zn, Pb, and Ni were detected in both sweet potatoes and spinach. Sweet potatoes exhibited higher concentrations of Cr, Fe, Cd, and Zn, whereas spinach showed higher concentrations of Ni, Cr, and Zn. The concentrations of essential elements in the vegetables analyzed in this study were within the safety baseline levels for human consumption. However, the concentrations of toxic elements exceeded the permissible limits established by the FAO/WHO. The elevated concentrations of toxic elements in sweet potatoes and spinach may be associated with contamination from municipal waste, chicken manure, motor vehicle emissions, or pesticides. Therefore, vegetable cultivation along roadsides in the Urban West Region of Zanzibar, as well as the use of fertilizers and chicken manure, may introduce high levels of heavy metals into the food chain.

This study aimed to investigate the genetic diversity of ‘Zuijinxiang’ grapes and their F1 mutant progeny to identify superior mutant plants with potential breeding value. Fifty mutant plants obtained from ‘Zuijinxiang’ grape seeds irradiated with 137Cs-γ rays were used as research materials. Genetic diversity and variation were analyzed using start codon targeted (SCoT) molecular markers, followed by cluster analysis. The results showed that: (1) The 36 SCoT primers generated abundant polymorphisms, with clearly distinguishable bands; both the amplification efficiency and polymorphism rate were 100%. (2) A total of 221 bands were amplified using the 36 primers, of which 175 were polymorphic. The average percentage of polymorphic bands was 80.3%, and the average genetic similarity coefficient was 0.916. (3) The number of observed alleles (Na) ranged from 4 to 8, with an average of 6.1389; the number of effective alleles (Ne) ranged from 1.2772 to 5.6322, with an average of 3.5968; expected heterozygosity (He) ranged from 0.2192 to 0.8344, with an average of 0.6965; observed heterozygosity (Ho) ranged from 0.1656 to 0.7808, with an average of 0.3035; Nei’s gene diversity index (H) ranged from 0.2170 to 0.8224, with an average of 0.6863; and the Shannon–Wiener index (I) ranged from 0.5186 to 1.8597, with an average of 1.4517. These findings indicate that the ‘Zuijinxiang’ radiation-induced mutant germplasm resources exhibit high genetic diversity. Furthermore, SCoT molecular marker technology can distinguish materials with close genetic distances and may be used for the early identification of grape mutants. This study provides a theoretical basis for developing superior mutant germplasm resources of ‘Zuijinxiang’ grapes.

### en-043
Silicon and proline play important physiological, metabolic, and functional roles in plants, particularly under water-deficit conditions. Their application can mitigate the adverse effects of stress on crops by improving water-use efficiency and antioxidant activity. This study aimed to evaluate the potential of silicon (Si) to alleviate the effects of water deficit in cowpea cultivars based on physiological, biochemical, and growth indicators. The experiment was conducted using a randomized block design with four cultivars (BRS Guariba, BRS Itaim, BRS Aracê, and BRS Rouxinol) and four irrigation treatments, with or without Si application (W100: 100% ETo; W50: 50% ETo; W50+100Si and W50+200Si: 50% ETo supplemented with 100 and 200 mg L-1 of silicon, respectively), in a 4 × 4 factorial scheme with five replicates. Leaf water potential, proline concentration, antioxidant enzyme activity, and plant growth indicators were evaluated. Under water-deficit conditions, all cultivars exhibited reduced leaf water potential, which compromised plant growth. However, Si applications at 200 mg L-1 in BRS Guariba and at 100 and 200 mg L-1 in BRS Itaim mitigated the effects of stress by increasing leaf water potential and ascorbate peroxidase activity in both cultivars. These treatments also increased proline concentration in BRS Guariba and reduced it in BRS Itaim, thereby maintaining plant growth. Although Si applications at 100 and 200 mg L-1 in BRS Rouxinol and BRS Aracê, respectively, did not increase leaf water potential, they reduced the detrimental effects of water stress on plant growth through the regulation of enzymatic metabolism and proline levels.

The number of electrons involved in the reduction indicated the formation of a nitro-anion radical, followed by its corresponding dianion. Subsistence fishing is a complex and heterogeneous concept within fisheries. Nevertheless, its connection to human protection makes it important to examine this practice from a human rights perspective. Using Chile as a case study because of its distinctive legal framework, this study aims to determine the scope of Chilean legislation on subsistence fishing by integrating international human rights treaties, case law, and reports from United Nations agencies. The analysis addresses three main issues. First, it examines the relationship between Chilean legislation and the right to food, as well as the promotion of decent social conditions. Second, it explains how the prohibition of riggings and propulsion systems helps identify economically vulnerable users and how this prohibition relates to vulnerability and poverty as human rights concepts. Finally, it demonstrates how the property rights of Indigenous peoples and the culture of fishing communities may require their inclusion and the consideration of their preferences in access to subsistence fishing resources. Based on the findings, we argue that human rights principles help clarify the concept of subsistence fishing and support partial amendments to Chilean legislation. More importantly, they introduce protective standards that allow this legislation to be understood not merely as an extension of state privilege but as an effort to promote equality through affirmative action. We conclude by presenting a conceptual approach to subsistence fishing in Chile, suggesting that it may help reveal new objectives and rights in fisheries and even influence how natural resources are allocated.

The bearing capacity of saturated soils can be estimated using effective- or total-stress approaches, extending the concepts proposed by Terzaghi (1943) and Skempton (1948), respectively. Recent studies have demonstrated that analogous approaches—the Modified Effective Stress Approach (MESA) and the Modified Total Stress Approach (MTSA)—can also be used to interpret and predict the bearing capacity of unsaturated soils by accounting for the influence of matric suction. However, the literature lacks a comprehensive discussion of the application of MESA and MTSA in practical geotechnical engineering. Accordingly, this state-of-the-art paper first introduces the background and underlying concepts of MESA and MTSA. It then revisits the analytical and numerical methods available in the literature for predicting the bearing capacity of unsaturated soils. These methods are categorized into two groups, corresponding to MESA and MTSA, and their applications are illustrated through examples. This paper aims not only to provide tools for a more systematic understanding of these approaches but also to improve the prediction of unsaturated soil bearing capacity and facilitate their adoption in geotechnical engineering practice.

### en-048
This study assessed mothers’ and fathers’ conceptualizations of joy, sadness, anger, fear, pride, and shame, as well as their beliefs about the importance of children expressing these emotions and the relationship between these beliefs and the profiles of autonomy, relatedness, and related autonomy. Sixty mother–father dyads with children up to three years of age participated in the study. Data were collected using questionnaires on parents’ conceptualizations of emotions. Most participants considered joy an important emotion for children of this age to express, associating it with individual-oriented motivation. In contrast, anger, pride, and shame were associated with older children. Mothers’ and fathers’ conceptualizations and beliefs did not differ. The autonomous-related self model was positively correlated with the importance that mothers and fathers attributed to all the emotions studied.

### en-050
In Brazil, thermotherapy of sugarcane setts is currently the primary management method for controlling Leifsonia xyli subsp. xyli (Lxx). During immersion, the enzymes and proteins of bacterial cells are denatured without damaging the sett buds. However, because the bacterium may escape detection and survive thermotherapy, potentially resulting in asymptomatic seedlings, this study aimed to detect Lxx in sugarcane setts using molecular techniques under different combinations of treatment times and temperatures, with or without the addition of antibiotics. Conventional PCR detected Lxx DNA only in the positive control, which consisted of a highly susceptible plant with a high bacterial concentration. In contrast, nested PCR detected Lxx DNA in all treatments. Thus, none of the thermotherapy treatments eliminated Lxx from the setts. The addition of kasugamycin also failed to eliminate the bacterium, although it reduced the bacterial population in the tested treatments. These findings confirm that nested PCR is a useful tool for detecting this phytobacterium in setts intended for use as seedlings.
