Meteorological systems that produce storms accompanied by strong winds, such as cold fronts and extratropical and subtropical cyclones, often affect sea conditions. Consequently, sea levels may rise exceptionally, causing coastal flooding. The years 2015 and 2016 were characterized by the occurrence of the positive phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation phenomenon, with very strong intensity. Southern Brazil experienced greater atmospheric instability, with frequent frontal system passages and the formation of extratropical cyclones, affecting sea conditions along the southern and southeastern coasts of Brazil. In this context, this study aimed to evaluate atmospheric conditions during storm surge events along the southern and southeastern coasts of Brazil during the 2015/2016 El Niño, using information from Severe Weather Warnings issued by the Brazilian Navy Hydrographic Center. Based on these warnings and ERA-Interim reanalysis data, distributions by maritime area, month, and season were analyzed, along with synoptic surface field analyses for the 12 most significant events. The results showed that autumn and winter recorded the highest occurrences of storm surges in both maritime areas during the 2015/2016 El Niño period. Overall, four atmospheric patterns favorable to storm surge events were identified, associated with the influence of extratropical cyclones and post-frontal high-pressure systems.

This article presents part of the research findings from a master's thesis that seeks to deepen reflections on librarians' professional practices and identify their perceptions and interpretations of professional activities and university libraries within the context of cyberculture. The study was conducted at a university library specializing in the medical field. It describes the library's history and characteristics and identifies the meanings attributed to librarianship practices based on semi-structured interviews with eight active or retired female librarians. The narratives reveal a persistent search for professional recognition and appreciation, as well as concern about maintaining users' attendance at the physical space of the library under study. Among the changes observed in professional practice, the role of "mediator" has increasingly given way to that of "educator," while the approach of "waiting for users" has been replaced by that of "seeking out users." These changes demonstrate concerns regarding the future of libraries and the role of librarians. The study also identifies meanings that may indicate strategies for preserving professional culture and maintaining a presence in the labor market.

### tr-034
The State of Ceará stands out in wind energy production due to its advantageous geographical position for wind power generation. This study presents the environmental, social, economic, and technological impacts arising from the implementation and operation of wind farms along the coast of Ceará through a comparative analysis of the Beberibe and Fleixeiras I Wind Farms. The main socio-environmental impacts identified were reductions in fauna and flora, deterioration of access roads, noise generation, changes to the natural landscape, restrictions on the right to freedom of movement, and cracks in houses. Economic impacts were more significant during the construction of the wind farms and gradually decreased over the years. Regarding technological aspects, the findings indicate that the greater the tower height and rotor diameter, the higher the amount of energy generated. This investigation was descriptive and exploratory in nature, examining these impacts through qualitative and quantitative methods. Targeted studies were necessary to understand the impacts caused by wind energy generation and to suggest alternatives for mitigating the worsening climate changes resulting from the greenhouse effect on the planet.

### tr-037
This article analyzes the road safety conditions of federal highways located in urban areas of southern Brazil. It seeks to assess the occurrence of traffic casualties based on records from the Federal Highway Police (PRF), in conjunction with pavement conditions, road geometry, and traffic signage, according to the methodology established by the National Transport Confederation (CNT). Critical urban road sections were identified using geoprocessing software. Accident classification was based on concepts grounded in the Safe System approach, which considers that traffic accidents, when they occur, should not result in serious injuries or fatalities. When fatalities and victims with serious injuries are considered, urban sections of federal highways in southern Brazil rank among the most dangerous in the country. Most highways in urban areas were classified as being in "good" condition in terms of maintenance; however, several sections recorded high numbers of casualties, particularly involving motorcycles. Among the most critical cases, São José, in the state of Santa Catarina, stood out as having the most hazardous road section. Nearly all segments with high accident rates were located near major urban agglomerations or regional capitals, probably due to the large volume of trips generated by socioeconomic activities.

The Cactaceae family comprises 1,850 species distributed primarily across the American continent, with Brazil ranking as the third-largest center of diversity for the family, with 277 species. Within this center of diversity, Boa Nova National Park (PNBN), located in eastern Bahia State, Brazil, covers approximately 12,065 ha and encompasses an east–west ecotone in which Atlantic Forest areas transition into mata-de-cipó vegetation and, farther west, into the Caatinga. As part of the objectives established by the 2011 National Action Plan for Cactus Conservation, this study presents a taxonomic survey of Cactaceae species occurring in PNBN, aiming to expand knowledge of the local flora and of conservation units in the State of Bahia. Plant material was collected during four field expeditions conducted over one year, along randomly selected trails covering the park's different environments. Ten genera and 15 species were recorded: Arrojadoa penicillata, Brasilicereus phaeacanthus, Brasiliopuntia brasiliensis, Cereus jamacaru, Cereus sp., Melocactus ernestii, Pereskia aculeata, P. bahiensis, Pilosocereus catingicola, P. pentaedrophorus subsp. robustus, Rhipsalis hileiabaiana, R. russellii, Stephanocereus leucostele, Tacinga funalis, and T. werneri. Within PNBN, mata-de-cipó vegetation and Caatinga showed the highest species richness, with eight and five species, respectively, whereas the Atlantic Forest contained only one species.

### tr-040
In the particleboard industry, most manufacturers use synthetic adhesives, particularly urea-formaldehyde-based adhesives. In this context, studies investigating the characteristics of alternative materials for the panel industry are important, especially when considering the final cost of the resulting products. The general objective of this study was to evaluate changes in the chemical structure of an adhesive that could potentially modify its bonding properties as a result of replacing urea-formaldehyde adhesive with lignosulfonate. To this end, the elemental chemical composition of lignosulfonate and its chemical bonds were determined using infrared spectroscopy (IR) and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), both in its pure form and in formulations containing different catalysts. The properties of urea-formaldehyde adhesive and the changes resulting from lignosulfonate addition were also evaluated, including pH, viscosity, and gelation time. NMR analysis revealed that lignosulfonate exhibited the same chemical bonding pattern generally observed in lignin derivatives. Elemental chemical analysis showed high levels of Mg, Ca, and Na. The addition of lignosulfonate to urea-formaldehyde adhesive caused acidification and reduced viscosity values. Gelation time was also affected; however, the methodology used for this type of polymer was found to require adjustments to the conventional method, including subjecting the material to temperatures above 90 °C. The results indicate that lignosulfonate can be used as a substitute for urea-formaldehyde adhesive in particleboard production.
