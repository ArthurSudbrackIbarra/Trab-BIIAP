Previous studies have investigated correction procedures that require learners to actively respond following an incorrect trial. This study directly investigated the effects of providing reinforcement after an active-response correction procedure during tact training in four children with autism. An echoic-to-tact training procedure was used to establish tacts. Embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS) is an important subtype of cryptogenic stroke that has attracted considerable attention due to ongoing clinical trials. We reviewed the discharge medical records of all patients who met the diagnostic criteria for ESUS and were treated at our institution between February 2016 and July 2017. Among 550 patients admitted with stroke, 51 were diagnosed with ESUS. The most prevalent risk factors were hypertension (60%), diabetes mellitus (34%), and smoking (36%). The mean National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores were 7 at admission and 4 at discharge, while the median modified Rankin Scale scores were 0 and 2 at admission and discharge, respectively. Compared with European and North American cohorts, our sample showed similar age distributions, prevalence of risk factors, and NIHSS scores at admission and discharge. Although based on a relatively small cohort, our findings suggest that the ESUS population exhibits similar characteristics across countries with different healthcare financing systems.
