Remote sensing can be used to estimate meteorological data. Using images of atmospheric variables obtained by the Atmospheric Infrared Sounder (AIRS) aboard the Aqua satellite, this study aimed to estimate daily maximum, minimum, and mean air temperatures in the Federal District region of Brazil. Simple and multiple linear regression models were developed using AIRS sensor data on surface temperature, relative humidity, and atmospheric water vapor as explanatory variables. These data were obtained at 12-hour intervals (daytime and nighttime) between January 2012 and December 2016. Observed air temperature values from two weather stations in the study region were used as dependent variables. The models yielded R² = 0.62 and RMSE = 1.64 °C for maximum temperature, R² = 0.59 and RMSE = 1.50 °C for minimum temperature, and R² = 0.76 and RMSE = 1.45 °C for mean temperature. It is concluded that AIRS sensor data are suitable for monitoring air temperature in the Federal District region.

This article presents findings from research on hybrid linguistic norms in New York Times news articles translated into Portuguese and published on the UOL website. According to Faraco (2008) and Bagno (2012), there is a distinction between the standard norm and the educated norm. The former comprises rules prescribing linguistic forms to be followed according to an idealized model of language that is difficult for most users to adhere to. The latter, in turn, comprises the set of linguistic forms actually used by many writers, which are therefore more intuitively accessible, even though they are not accepted by the conservative grammatical tradition represented by the standard norm, commonly disseminated through grammar books and style manuals. The study seeks to assess whether it is possible to argue that journalistic texts translated from English, like journalistic texts originally written in Portuguese, which have already been shown to exhibit this characteristic, are similarly receptive to linguistic forms that are not sanctioned by the standard norm.
