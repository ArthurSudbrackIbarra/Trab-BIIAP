Seed coats and water content may influence seed tolerance to thermal shocks, as may their environment of origin. To investigate these possibilities, seeds of Plathymenia reticulata and Stryphnodendron adstringens were collected in northern Minas Gerais State (representing dry savanna) and northeastern Mato Grosso State (representing humid savanna) within the Cerrado biome. Their responses to thermal shocks at 110, 140, and 170 °C for 2.5 minutes were evaluated under different conditions: intact, scarified, and scarified and subsequently soaked. Scarified and soaked seeds germinated more rapidly in both species. Seeds from dry savanna had lower initial water content and slower germination in both species, but greater tolerance to thermal shocks than seeds from humid savanna. P. reticulata seeds did not survive treatment at 140 °C, whereas S. adstringens seeds from dry savanna withstood treatment at 170 °C, regardless of whether they had been scarified and/or soaked. We conclude that P. reticulata seeds are less tolerant to thermal shocks than S. adstringens seeds, that dry seeds are more tolerant to thermal shocks than soaked seeds, and that seeds from dry savannas are more tolerant to thermal shocks than those from humid environments.

In contrast, ALAS2 expression was progressively downregulated, inversely proportional to doxorubicin concentrations.

**Conclusion:** Increased ALAS1 expression may play a role in the elevation of heme levels when H9c2 cardiomyocytes are exposed to doxorubicin and may represent a therapeutic target for doxorubicin-induced myocardial toxicity. (Arq Bras Cardiol. 2021; 116(2):315-322)

Oxytocin was extracted from plasma using a C-18 chromatographic column and quantified using the commercial Oxytocin EIA kit.
