In this study, a statistical downscaling model (SDM) based on the analog methodology was used to project future precipitation and temperature patterns in the city of Maceió, on the eastern coast of Northeast Brazil (9.7° S; 35.7° W; 64.5 m), and to assess their impacts on sugarcane yield. Historical observed data were used for downscaling precipitation and temperature using different global climate models (CanESM2, CNRM-CM5, IPSL-CM5A-MR, GFDL-ESM2M, MIROC-ESM, MPI-ESM-MR, and NorESM1-M) for the reference period (1961-1990) and then for future scenarios from 2021 to 2080. Contrary to expectations, the combination of reduced rainfall and increased evapotranspiration in the future scenarios did not decrease the yield estimate, which is projected to increase. This can be attributed to the fact that the projected reduction in rainfall, more pronounced in the rainy season, will not be decisive for a decline in yield, since most climate models predict an increase in rainfall in the dry period from September to December, which is critical for the initial growth of the crop, whose planting calendar begins in the first ten-day period of September.

Juglans regia and Carya illinoinensis, both of the family Juglandaceae, are walnut and pecan species known worldwide for the production of nuts, with a growing expansion of cultivated area in South America. However, in recent years there has been an increase in the incidence of diseases in these species, which compromises the profitability of the crops and the quality of the product. Colletotrichum nymphaeae was recently identified in Brazil causing damage to Juglans regia fruits. This study aimed to evaluate the cross-infection potential of C. nymphaeae, isolated from symptomatic Juglans regia fruits, on leaflets and fruits of Carya illinoinensis cultivars. For this purpose, culture medium discs containing pathogen structures were placed on leaflets and fruits of six and three Carya illinoinensis cultivars, respectively. Colletotrichum nymphaeae proved to be pathogenic on both leaflets and fruits of all cultivars evaluated, with the largest lesions on leaflets observed in the cultivars 'Barton', 'Melhorada' and 'Imperial', whereas the cultivar 'Shawnee' showed lower susceptibility to the pathogen. On fruits, the cultivar 'Imperial' was more susceptible to the pathogen, as it showed the largest lesions.
