### en-031
This article situates South Tyrol among other multinational states, such as Flanders, Québec and Catalonia, in an attempt to broaden models of multilevel governance, especially with regard to the accommodation of migrants. It also examines how language policies affect migrants settled in this Italian multinational context, drawing on data from semi-structured interviews that capture migrants' perspectives on the policy of declaration of linguistic affiliation or aggregation and on its effects on social division. Participants also raised the topics of racism and xenophobia in connection with linguistic issues involving the non-legitimation of the speaker. The findings show that migrants perceive the declaration policy as a burden: some of the participants stated that they do not feel represented by it, and that it reinforces social division.

### en-032
This study discusses the possibilities of adopting digital resources for teaching and learning English as a second language (L2) in higher education in general and at a public university in Brazil in particular. Key aspects of the literature review, which covers approaches such as Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL), Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) and English-Medium Instruction (EMI), are compared with the context and activities of a university in the Global South in order to discuss the use of digital resources in L2 English teaching and learning. The results suggest that a critical use of technologies is necessary to promote a more balanced and sustainable language teaching and learning process. Institutional support is also essential for teachers and students to develop the skills required in an increasingly interconnected world. We conclude that a combination of alternative practices, supported by digital resources, can foster effective language teaching and learning in higher education institutions.

### en-033
Background: Procedures that reduce errors during repertoire learning play an important role in Applied Behavior Analysis for people with autism, given the detrimental effects that excessive exposure to error may have on learning. Previous studies have investigated the effects of correction procedures that require an active student response after an error trial. Some intervention manuals recommend against reinforcing responses after correction to prevent the establishment of prompt dependence. This study directly investigated the effect of reinforcement after an active-response correction procedure during tact training in four children with autism. An echoic-to-tact training procedure was used to teach tacts. A "no reinforcement after correction" (NRC) condition was compared with a "reinforcement after correction" (RC) condition using an adapted alternating treatments design. Results: All participants required fewer correction trials in the RC condition than in the NRC condition. Across all 26 sessions in which both training procedures were implemented, participants' performance was higher with RC than with NRC in 17 sessions and was the same in 3 sessions. Conclusions: We discuss the effectiveness of reinforcing correct responding after an active-response correction procedure, the absence of prompt dependence, and the implications of better correction procedures for applied settings.

### en-034
The Brazilian semiarid region is historically characterized by a scarcity of good-quality water. Although groundwater reserves are abundant in this region, these waters have high concentrations of dissolved salts, which makes them unsuitable for irrigating most crops. The objective of this study was to evaluate the growth, production and salinity tolerance of three cauliflower cultivars grown in NFT (Nutrient Film Technique) hydroponics and subjected to different electrical conductivities of the nutrient solution (ECsol) prepared with saline water. The experiment was carried out in a randomized block design with a split-plot arrangement and six replications. Plants were subjected to six ECsol levels (1.94 [control], 3.24, 4.10, 5.04, 5.92 and 7.01 dS m⁻¹) in the main plots, and three cauliflower cultivars ('Piracicaba de Verão', 'Sabrina' and 'SF1758'), grown in the same hydroponic channel, were allocated to the subplots. Vegetative growth, inflorescence production and cultivar salinity tolerance were evaluated. In general, except for the number of leaves, leaf width, and leaf and shoot fresh matter, the ECsol levels negatively affected the vegetative growth and inflorescence yield of the cauliflower cultivars. The cultivars 'Piracicaba de Verão' and 'Sabrina' were considered moderately sensitive to salinity, whereas 'SF1758' was considered moderately tolerant.

### en-035
Objective: To investigate the use of health services and limitations in performing usual activities among adults and older adults with and without noncommunicable chronic diseases (NCDs), according to sociodemographic strata. Methods: This cross-sectional study analyzed data from the 2019 National Health Survey. The final sample comprised 88,531 households in which interviews were completed, referring to individuals aged 18 years and older. The prevalence of service use in the population with NCDs was compared with that in the population without NCDs and stratified by socioeconomic and demographic variables. Prevalence ratios (PRs) and 95% confidence intervals (95%CI) were calculated. Results: In 2019, 47.6% (95%CI 47.0–48.3) of the population reported having one or more NCDs. Compared with the population without NCDs, those with NCDs had more medical consultations in the previous 12 months (adjusted PR [APR]=1.21; 95%CI 1.20–1.23), used more health services in the previous 2 weeks (APR=2.01; 95%CI 1.91–2.11), had more hospitalizations (APR=2.11; 95%CI 1.89–2.36), and had more limitations in performing usual activities (APR=2.52; 95%CI 2.30–2.76). A positive dose–response gradient was observed between the number of comorbidities and the use of services. In all socioeconomic and demographic strata, the prevalence of the indicators was higher in people with NCDs. Conclusion: The presence of NCDs was associated with greater use of health services (i.e., consultations, service use, and hospitalization) and with greater restriction of usual activities in all socioeconomic and demographic strata.

### en-036
Brazilian Bill n. 6,299/2002, which addresses pesticide regulation, represents an attempt to loosen and weaken the regulation of these substances, threatening the rights to food and to a healthy environment enshrined in the Constitution of the Federative Republic of Brazil. This article reviews the bill and its most problematic provisions through the lenses of food and nutrition security and of the principles and provisions of national environmental law. Against this background, the paper concludes that Bill n. 6,299/2002 undermines previously achieved progress in providing alternative policy pathways for sustainable agriculture in Brazil. It conflicts with environmental law principles, neglecting precaution and prevention and representing a regression in protection levels, while undermining the human rights to food and to a healthy environment. The methodology comprises a literature and documentary review combined with inductive reasoning.

### en-038
Embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS) is an important subgroup of cryptogenic strokes that has recently received attention owing to ongoing trials. We reviewed the medical records, at discharge from the stroke unit, of all patients who met ESUS criteria and were treated at our institution between February 2016 and July 2017. Among 550 stroke patients, 51 had ESUS. Hypertension (60%), diabetes mellitus (34%) and smoking (36%) were the most prevalent risk factors. The mean National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores were 7 at admission and 4 at discharge, while the median modified Rankin Scale scores were 0 at admission and 2 at discharge. Our sample showed ages, risk factor prevalence and NIHSS scores at admission and discharge similar to those of European and North American cohorts. Although our cohort was small, our findings suggest that the ESUS population is similar across countries with different health financing systems.

### en-039
This article aims to identify the changes in corporate governance associated with the life cycle of companies. It is guided by the research problem of understanding the effects of business maturity on ownership structure, corporate relations and other governance configurations. The study used a longitudinal analysis of the changes that occurred at Transportadora Brasileira Gasoduto Bolívia-Brasil SA (TBG), a privately held company controlled by Petrobras Logística de Gás, with the participation of several state and private partners. Data were collected through document analysis and interviews with executives. The results identified a relationship between the stages of the business and the characteristics of its investors, ranging from an initial phase built around energy companies specialized in the business to a maturity phase in which the investment became attractive to capitalist partners interested in financial returns. In addition, planning before capital consolidation, the existence of a financing structure, clear criteria for selecting partners, and the presence of a second relevant shareholder mitigate problems of abuse of power or non-compliance with established rules, helping to preserve the relationship between shareholders over time and the sustainability of corporate agreements. The study contributes a new perspective on the analysis of corporate governance, considering the changes and adaptations required over time and offering reflections on corporate structures and the priorities of governance actors.

### en-040
In his contribution to the Special Issue "Digital and semiotic mechanisms of contemporary populisms," Jan Blommaert offers a communicability model that accounts for political discourse (and other discourses) in the post-digital era. He begins by arguing that the idea of the public as a homogeneous entity, which was very popular in the twentieth-century sociological imagination of how propaganda worked in "manufacturing consent," can no longer explain the fragmented audiences of the post-digital era. He illustrates his argument with the circulation of political tweets and retweets as texts in an algorithm-oriented world, a circulation that targets niche audiences. In the final section, he argues that discourse analysts need to operate from this communicability model if they are to understand the crucial role of political discourse in contemporary social life.
