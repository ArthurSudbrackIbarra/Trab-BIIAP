### en-021
This study aimed to assess the level of disclosure of the survival, growth, and profitability (SGP) construct in the mission statements of Brazilian companies and in the collective discourse of different economic sectors, classified according to the "Biggest & Best" yearbook published by Exame Magazine. It addresses a gap in the literature by examining the disclosure of the SGP construct in the mission statements of large Brazilian companies. Because the mission statement is the genesis of strategic planning, analyzing the disclosure of the SGP construct allows us to discuss how relevant the mission statement is to the elaboration, implementation, and monitoring of that planning. The benefit of a mission statement aligned with strategic planning lies in communicating long-term SGP goals adequately to stakeholders, on the assumption that comprehensive and objective communication minimizes the risk of failures during the management process. Collective subject discourse (CSD) was used to develop a metric for qualifying the constitutive elements of organizational mission statements, making it possible to identify whether Brazilian companies, grouped into different economic sectors, smooth, conceal, or omit the construct formed by the SGP components in their mission statements. We investigated the presence or absence of the SGP components in the collective discourse of the mission statements of 220 large Brazilian companies. Most of the companies in the sample do not include SGP in their mission statements, and those that do address it with discursive vagueness, lacking clarity in the disclosure of its components. For academia, this finding contributes to understanding the constituent components of the mission statements of large Brazilian companies. For organizations, the findings indicate the need to reflect on the content used to formulate their mission statements.

### en-022
Cover crop straw may alter microbial activity and populations, with repercussions for environmental change and for C and N dynamics, thereby providing important information for planning appropriate land use in the Cerrado. The objective of this study was to evaluate the effect of cover crop straw on soil microbial attributes and organic matter quality. Experimental units consisted of 100 g of sieved soil placed in small plastic cups and mixed with straw from cover plants. Treatments were distributed in a completely randomized design (CRD) with a 7 × 7 + 1 factorial scheme, comprising seven types of straw evaluated at 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, 63, and 105 days after incubation, plus a control without straw, with three replicates. The metabolic quotient (qCO2) ranged from 0.1 (Crotalaria spectabilis at 14 days) to 5.1% (Cajanus cajan 'IAPAR 43' at 42 days). The organic carbon and nitrogen reservoirs were affected differently by legume and grass straw across the evaluation dates. The incorporation of Brachiaria brizantha and Mucuna aterrima had a positive impact on the microbial attributes (qCO2, Cmic, and Nmic) up to the 21st day of incubation, with little loss of carbon dioxide and growth of the microbial population. All the species evaluated showed potential for use in crop rotation systems, enabling no-tillage systems in the Cerrado of Piauí. However, species should be selected according to specific purposes. Thus, the use of cover crops is an important tool for improving the biological quality of soils in the Brazilian northeastern Cerrado.

### en-023
Aim: To report the occurrence of Apistogrammoides pucallpaensis in Brazilian territory, as its known distribution has so far been restricted to Peru and Colombia. Methods: Specimens were collected in 2013 during an ichthyofauna survey of the Auati-Paraná region, located in the Mamirauá Reserve, state of Amazonas. Nets were used in the floating aquatic vegetation, and rapichés (hand nets) were used in the coastal region. Apistogrammoides is the only genus among the Geophaginae with 6–9 spines in the anal fin, whereas Apistogramma has 3–4. Results: The specimens showed three evident marks on the caudal fin and a black stripe on the side of the body running from the eye to the caudal peduncle; these features distinguish the genus. Conclusions: This study extends the known distribution of A. pucallpaensis and constitutes the first record of the species for Brazil.

### en-024
Given the growing volume of research on Thysanoptera in Brazil, studies have been conducted to improve our understanding of thrips diversity in areas where the fauna has historically been neglected. Accordingly, we recorded the diversity of thrips (Insecta: Thysanoptera) associated with a restinga fragment on the campus of the State University of Ceará (UECE), Fortaleza, Ceará state, and calculated estimated richness and diversity indices. Samples were collected from 2011 to 2013 from the flowers of 86 plant species. The material was taken to the Laboratory of Insect-Plant Interaction, where thrips were screened under a stereomicroscope. We collected 456 adults and 58 immatures, representing 14 species, in addition to one unidentified species of Treherniella. Microcephalothrips abdominalis was found on a large number of host plants, and Frankliniella insularis was the most common species. About two-thirds of the total thrips species richness was associated with three plant families (Amaranthaceae, Caesalpiniaceae, and Poaceae), and six thrips species were each associated with only one plant species. The observed species richness was close to that estimated by the Bootstrap and Jackknife 1 estimators. The Shannon-Wiener (H') and Simpson (D) diversity indices were 1.7607 and 0.7769, respectively. Although the species are common, 46 new associations between plant species and thrips were established, 13 of which are true host associations, demonstrating the importance of coastal vegetation in maintaining thrips populations.

### en-025
In this paper, we elaborate on the consequences of a post-humanist perspective for the problem of physical disability by approaching the use of assistive technologies (ATs) by disabled people as the introduction of a low-tech cyborg into the world. In doing so, we highlight examples of communication ATs and draw analogies between ATs and languages in the constitution of selves and social contexts. ATs are ideologically informed; they can be seen either as a way to "fix" an "impaired" person or as a strategy to overcome a physical and social context that disables some people and makes others "able-bodied." We argue that becoming a low-tech cyborg can be a form of social inclusion if disability is understood as produced by the context rather than as an inherent dysfunctionality of the individual. Based on this assumption, we identify two strategies of social inclusion for the low-tech cyborg: disembodiment of the Self and embodied virtuality. We note, however, that low-tech cyborgs can be configured out of necessity or by choice, and that the same socioeconomic factors that produce inequality in general also operate in the social exclusion or inclusion of the low-tech cyborg. Thus, ATs can be adopted and transformed by choice in ways that widen the gap between cyborg haves and have-nots, while both kinds of cyborgs may become increasingly subject to cognitive and affective exploitation in the context of cognitive capitalism. We conclude that the potential of a post-humanist perspective on disability lies not in making "impaired humans" whole, nor in making "whole humans" more than human, but in keeping selves ethically connected with others, whether through virtual embodiment or embodied virtuality.

### en-026
The design of water distribution systems involves, above all, determining the pipe size that meets the system's requirements while minimizing annual installation and operation costs. Among the existing methodologies for determining the economic pipe diameter, Bresse's equation remains common among designers. This work analyzes the efficiency of Bresse's equation, the MBPW, and the MLVPC by comparing them with the MREC. We recommend that designers not use the MBPW or the MLVPC. For small water distribution systems, Bresse's equation can be used, provided that an appropriate value is adopted for its coefficient k. For HDPE, we propose k = 1.17 or k = 1.18. For PVC DEFOFO and GFRP, we suggest ranges of 1.19 to 1.23 and 1.29 to 1.32, respectively. For larger water distribution systems, we recommend the MREC as the methodology for determining the economic pipe diameter, because an appropriate value for Bresse's coefficient cannot be established.

### en-027
Aim: To evaluate the general state of research on the decomposition of organic matter in aquatic environments in the subtropical region of South America. Methods: We compiled publications (1990–2018) using a combination of terms referring to decomposition, detritus, aquatic environments, and South American countries with subtropical climates. The characteristics of the publications were analyzed over time. Results: A total of 1,042 publications were found, of which 106 specifically addressed the purpose of this study. For subtropical South America, this research topic is incipient. Most studies were carried out in streams (39.6%) and addressed the decomposition process and associated invertebrates (42.5%). Studies in lentic environments (e.g., lakes, 10%) are markedly scarce. Conclusions: Studies on the decomposition of organic detritus in aquatic ecosystems in the subtropical region of South America are still emerging. While the role of shredders is moderately well understood, the importance of microorganisms in the decomposition process still needs to be better evaluated. Finally, our study showed that scientific collaboration boosted publication on the topic.

### en-028
This paper introduces PeMSyn, a free tool intended to aid the design and simulation of permanent magnet synchronous machines. Using PeMSyn, one can easily design a permanent magnet synchronous machine, define its winding distribution, and assess its performance. Machine performance is evaluated by finite element analysis (FEA), as PeMSyn provides an interface between Matlab and FEMM, allowing several machine parameters to be obtained. Another notable characteristic of PeMSyn is its modularity: each functionality can be run independently, and new functionalities and machine topologies can be added either by the user or through a PeMSyn update. To be simple and user-friendly, PeMSyn was coded in Matlab as a graphical user interface (GUI). Moreover, users can easily translate all labels and variable names into their native language using a language description file, already available in English and Portuguese. The modules, the machine design process, the equations involved, and an application are presented in this paper. To provide guidelines on how to use PeMSyn, an example of machine design is proposed and its performance is assessed.

### en-029
The main objective of this research was to evaluate the reading time of street names on dynamic egocentrically referenced maps for In-Car Route Guidance and Navigation Systems. Two road network patterns (grid-like and non-grid-like) were considered, and tasks were devised to search for street names in two route segments (where the driver was and where the driver would have to maneuver). In the non-grid-like road network pattern, for the task of searching for the name of a road where the driver would maneuver, street names aligned horizontally and next to the route were read significantly faster than street names positioned along the route. Street names positioned obliquely required more reading time than street names aligned horizontally. Dynamic egocentrically referenced maps should present street names horizontally aligned and next to the route for the segment where the driver will maneuver, for both road network patterns. In addition, street names should be positioned next to the car symbol.

### en-030
The Annonaceae is one of the most anatomically and structurally uniform plant families. Chemotaxonomy is a common practice for determining chemical patterns within such families at different phylogenetic levels. The aim of this study was to build a dataset of all secondary metabolites isolated from the Annonaceae family and to perform the respective chemotaxonomic analysis using self-organizing maps (SOMs). The dataset comprises 5321 botanical occurrences and 1860 unique molecules present in all subfamilies of the Annonaceae. Diterpenes account for 366 unique compounds and 533 botanical occurrences, found in both the Annonoideae and Malmeoideae subfamilies. The tribes Annoneae, Xylopieae, and Miliuseae had the highest numbers of botanical occurrences and were therefore selected for analysis. Molecular descriptors of the diterpenes and their respective botanical occurrences were used to generate the SOMs. These SOMs showed clear and indicative separation of the tribes, with a match rate higher than 70%. Our results corroborate the morphological and molecular data. These models can be used to predict the phylogenetic location of certain diterpenes and to accelerate research on Annonaceae secondary metabolites and their biological potential.
