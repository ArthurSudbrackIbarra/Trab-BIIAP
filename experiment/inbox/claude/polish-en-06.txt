### en-051
In this paper, we first analyze the atmosphere as a gas mixture per unit mass governed by the Van der Waals equation, considering the main components of air and their respective critical properties (critical temperature, TC, and critical pressure, pC). After adjusting the corresponding constants, which we denote I and D, we obtain the Van der Waals equation of state for the atmosphere in this context. Next, we analyze the order of magnitude of the terms in this equation and propose a Van der Waals-like equation of state that depends only on D, which we call the WD equation of state. In addition, we consider a physical approach to the Van der Waals equation for the atmosphere, studying the pressure terms associated with intermolecular repulsive and attractive forces in air, and we again obtain the WD equation of state. With this new proposal, we verify that the potential temperature and the equivalent potential temperature retain the same expressions as those established in atmospheric thermodynamics under the ideal gas law. However, we find corrections that depend on D in both the alternative form of the first law of thermodynamics and the virtual temperature.

### en-052
Objective: To analyze the association between the occurrence of new tuberculosis cases and the Adapted Living Conditions Index, and to describe the spatial distribution of the disease in an endemic municipality. Method: This analytical ecological study was based on new cases among residents of an endemic municipality in the North Region of Brazil. Data were obtained from the Notifiable Diseases Information System and the 2010 Demographic Census. The Adapted Living Conditions Index was derived by factor analysis, and its association with the occurrence of the disease was assessed using the chi-square test, with the type I error set at 0.05. Kernel estimation was used to describe the density of tuberculosis in each census sector. Results: The incidence coefficient was 97.5 per 100,000 inhabitants. There was a statistically significant association between the number of cases and socioeconomic class, with membership in the highest economic class reducing the chance of disease occurrence. Thematic maps showed that tuberculosis was heterogeneously distributed, with a concentration in the southern region of the municipality. Conclusion: The association of tuberculosis with precarious living conditions reinforces the importance of discussing social determinants in the health-disease process, in order to support equitable health actions in risk areas in contexts of vulnerability.

### en-053
Bottom-dwelling elasmobranchs, such as guitarfishes, skates and stingrays, are highly susceptible to bycatch because their distributions overlap with areas of fishing operations. Catch data for this group are also often merged into generic categories, which prevents species-specific assessments. Along the east coast of Australia, the Eastern Fiddler Ray, Trygonorrhina fasciata (Müller & Henle, 1841), and the Sydney Skate, Dentiraja australis (Macleay, 1884), are common components of bycatch, yet little information is available on their age, growth and reproductive timing, which makes impact assessment difficult. In this study, we estimate and report age and growth (from vertebral bands) as well as reproductive parameters for these two species, based on 171 Eastern Fiddler Rays (100 females and 71 males) and 81 Sydney Skates (47 females and 34 males). Based on von Bertalanffy growth curve fits, Eastern Fiddler Rays grew to larger sizes than Sydney Skates but did so more slowly (ray: L∞ = 109.61, t0 = 0.26 and K = 0.20; skate: L∞ = 51.95, t0 = −0.99 and K = 0.34; sexes combined). Both species had higher liver weight ratios (HSI) during the austral summer. Gonadal weight ratios (GSI) were higher in the austral winter for Eastern Fiddler Rays and in the austral spring for Sydney Skates.

### en-054
The debate on climate change has begun to influence public policy through technical documents that include predictions and impacts concerning the environment, society, the economy and culture. In this context, research and intellectual contributions on the theme have focused on developing mechanisms to avoid or mitigate the approaching global crisis. In this review, taking a somewhat different perspective, we present the work of Frank Fischer, who, accepting the inevitability of the crisis, adopts a line of political debate on "the place of democracy" in a context of established global disaster. We highlight his main arguments, including the authoritarian tendencies that emerge as a means of confronting the crisis, as well as the structures and mechanisms for defending democracy, and we bring into the discussion perspectives on eco-localism and its associated elements. We also analyze the potential and some of the weaknesses of Fischer's arguments. Fischer is an author who has become established as a fundamental reference for those engaged with the subject.

### en-055
Development in glycosylation methods: a key to access applications in the synthesis of bioactive molecules. Glycosylation is an important class of reactions in organic chemistry, and the development of new methods contributes to the synthesis of many biologically active compounds containing various glycosidic bonds. It is arguably the most important, albeit challenging, reaction in carbohydrate chemistry. Examples of glycosylation products include glycoproteins, glycolipids, glycosaminoglycans, oligosaccharides and polysaccharides. Glycosylation types are classified according to the identity of the atom that links the carbohydrate chain, namely C-linked, N-linked, O-linked or S-linked. In this short review, we describe recent reports on the main glycosylation methods, their basic mechanisms, the factors influencing stereoselectivity, and their applications in the synthesis of bioactive molecules.

### en-056
Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a disease characterized by progressive loss of muscle fibers, advancing gradually from proximal to distal muscles. Although a few studies have investigated hand grip strength in non-ambulatory patients with DMD, the literature on its relationship with functional capacity is lacking. Objective: To determine the associations between hand grip strength and functional measures in non-ambulatory children with DMD. Methods: Hand grip strength was evaluated using a dynamometer in children with DMD. The children were also assessed with the Turkish version of the Egen Klassifikation Scale Version 2 (EK2) for global functional capacity, the Performance of Upper Limb (PUL) for upper limb functional performance, and the ABILHAND-Kids for hand ability. Results: The mean age of the 38 children with DMD was 12.02 ± 1.99 years. Dominant hand grip strength was higher than non-dominant hand grip strength (p < 0.05). The EK2, PUL and ABILHAND-Kids scores were 13.02 ± 5.50, 49.86 ± 14.34 and 26.81 ± 7.59, respectively. Hand grip strength was correlated with the EK2 score (p < 0.05). Conclusions: Measuring functional ability and strength in very weak children with DMD is known to be difficult and complex for therapists and clinicians in the clinical setting. Although the correlation is moderate, hand grip strength may be used in clinical practice as a practical assessment tool to gain immediate insight into global functional capacity in non-ambulatory children with DMD.

### en-057
Introduction: The integrity of articular cartilage determines the functional state of the joint. In recent years, the development of MRI sequences for the assessment of various articular cartilages has become the focus of many research topics. Objective: To retrospectively study the diagnostic accuracy of three-dimensional MRI sequences for knee cartilage injury caused by sports-related trauma. Methods: Forty-six knee joints of 45 patients with sports injuries underwent multi-sequence MRI before surgery, including conventional knee MRI (SE T1WI, FSE PD/T2WI), 3D SPGR and 3D FIESTA sequences. Results: According to the surgical findings, 3D SPGR combined with conventional MRI sequences achieved the highest sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value and negative predictive value in the evaluation of cartilage damage: 73%, 98%, 95% and 90%, respectively. Conclusions: 3D SPGR combined with conventional MRI sequences can improve the accuracy of evaluation and diagnosis of cartilage disease within a reasonable scan time. Level of evidence II; Therapeutic studies – investigation of treatment results.

### en-058
Bartonella is a genus of emerging zoonotic bacteria mainly associated with mammalian erythrocytes and endothelial cells. Bats are natural reservoirs of a variety of important pathogens that affect human and animal health. Recent reports have highlighted the role of bats and bat flies in the maintenance of Bartonella. Here, we show that none of the 29 bat blood DNA samples obtained from five bat species on São Luís Island, state of Maranhão, northeastern Brazil, was positive for Bartonella in qPCR assays targeting nuoG. In contrast, three of 15 DNA samples (20%) from flies of the family Streblidae were positive for Bartonella. BLASTn results showed that the gltA and rpoB sequences shared identities ranging from 97.2% to 100% with Bartonella sequences amplified from bats or bat flies from Costa Rica and Brazil. These findings were supported by phylogenetic analyses based on Bayesian inference. The present study shows that Bartonella genotypes are present in bat flies, thus shedding light on the distribution of bat fly-related Bartonella genotypes in South America.

### en-059
The current study analyzes Chilean students' and parents' perceptions of English language assessment. A total of 352 Chilean 10th graders were surveyed using the Perceptions of the Assessment Tasks Inventory, the Perceived Classroom Assessment Environment Scale and the Test Anxiety Scale to measure their perceptions of English language assessment. A semi-structured interview was also conducted to capture the perceptions of 74 parents regarding English and the way their children are assessed in the English subject. The quantitative data were analyzed using the Pearson correlation coefficient to measure the strength of the relationships among scores on the three scales, and the qualitative data were categorized using content and frequency analysis. The findings suggest that the subscales of the three surveys are significantly related and that students feel a learning-oriented assessment environment benefits them more than a performance-oriented one. The former favors students' learning and mastery of subject materials, whereas the latter aligns with classroom assessment practices that emphasize grades over learning. The qualitative results also suggest that parents have a positive perception of the English language and believe it is useful for their children's lives, even though they may themselves find it difficult to learn.

### en-060
Context: While the digital economy requires a new generation of technology for scientists and practitioners, the software engineering (SE) field faces a gender crisis. SE research is a global enterprise that requires the participation of both genders for the advancement of science and evidence-based practice. However, women across the world tend to be significantly underrepresented in such research, frequently receiving less funding than men and appearing less often as authors of research publications. Data on this phenomenon remain sparse and incomplete; in particular, in evidence-based software engineering (EBSE), no studies have analyzed the participation of women. Objective: To present the results of a systematic mapping study (SM) conducted to collect and evaluate evidence on female researchers who have contributed to the area of EBSE. Method: We performed the SM by manually searching the major EBSE conferences and journals. We identified 981 studies, of which 183 were authored or co-authored by women and were therefore included. Results: Contributions from women to secondary studies have increased globally over the years but remain concentrated in European countries. Additionally, collaboration among research groups is still fragile and relies on a few women acting as bridges. Latin American researchers contribute substantially to the field, although they do not collaborate extensively within their own region. Conclusions: The findings of this study are expected to add to existing knowledge about women's contributions to the EBSE area. We hope that our results prompt reflection on the gender issue and motivate actions and policies to attract female researchers to this area.
