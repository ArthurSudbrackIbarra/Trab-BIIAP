OBJECTIVE: To evaluate the effect of a hot shower and perineal exercise with a Swiss ball, alone and combined, on the perception of pain, anxiety, and labor progression. METHOD: Randomized controlled clinical trial with 128 parturient women allocated to three therapy groups: shower, ball, and the two combined. The perception of pain and anxiety was assessed before and thirty minutes after the therapeutic intervention using a visual analog scale (VAS). RESULTS: There was an increase in the pain score and a reduction in anxiety in all groups, especially when the shower was used. Cervical dilation increased in all intervention groups (p<.001), as did the number of uterine contractions, particularly among those who used the shower and ball combined, who also showed a shorter duration of labor. Although the themes of environmental management maturity levels in business and the adoption of GSCM practices are becoming consolidated, they have so far traveled a long way disconnected from each other, even though both have their origins in corporate sustainability. Through multiple cases, this study sought to characterize a logical pattern in the adoption of GSCM practices inherent to each maturity level in environmental management. Based on the study of five cases of companies in high environmental impact production chains (the agricultural pesticide supply sector, the batteries sector, and the lead recycling sector), the study sought to identify the relationship between Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) practices and environmental management maturity levels. To date, this article is the first study to relate environmental management maturity levels to GSCM practices, applied to five high-environmental-impact cases in Brazil.

Orchidaceae is the largest family of vascular plants and is a group under intense threat due to human activity. Cattleya caulescens and C. endsfeldzii are rupicolous species endemic to Minas Gerais, Brazil, and appear on state and national red lists of threatened species. Accordingly, morpho-anatomical studies were conducted with the aim of increasing knowledge of these taxa, given the potential these studies have for species management. Cattleya caulescens and C. endsfeldzii show strong morpho-anatomical similarities, which supports previous studies that place both in the same section of the genus. Several anatomical characteristics described here (uniseriate epidermis, hypostomatic leaf, large primary pit fields in the chlorophyll parenchyma, and root anatomy) are common to many other Orchidaceae species. The xeromorphic characters observed are related to edaphoclimatic conditions, since the plants occur on rocky substrate, under constant exposure to sunlight and severe water restriction for most of the year. This work contributes to expanding knowledge of the genus Cattleya and to assisting the taxonomic delimitation of these threatened species, which occur in areas of intense human activity.

### tr-030
The general objective of this study was to analyze the physical characteristics of Mesoscale Convective Complexes (MCCs) in the Northeast region of Brazil between 2008 and 2017. To produce short-term weather forecasts, it is necessary to understand and describe these systems in detail, as MCCs often cause adverse phenomena, leading to socioeconomic losses for society as a whole. The identification and selection of cases, as well as all calculations of their physical characteristics, were carried out with the aid of an algorithm for the automatic analysis and detection of MCCs, based on the criteria proposed by Maddox. METEOSAT satellite images in the enhanced infrared channel were used as the input for the algorithm's processing. Sixty-five systems were identified, occurring especially in the autumn and summer seasons. The MCCs began to develop between late night and early morning (between 00 and 03 UTC). There was an interval of approximately four hours between genesis and maximum development, when they reach their maximum areal extent. The average coverage area of these MCCs was approximately 120,000 km², with one case recorded with a total area of around 427,926 km².
